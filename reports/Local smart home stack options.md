# Reuse the edge, build the brain

As of November 2026 a fully local, subscription-free smart home is built in one of four ways: on Home Assistant, on another established hub such as openHAB, on a self-built hub that reuses ESPHome and Zigbee2MQTT at the device edge, or from scratch. The evidence rules out the last, finds no ready local-model voice path in the second, and leaves a real trade-off between the first and the third. For this household the self-built Python hub on Mosquitto is the defensible choice, because ESPHome and Zigbee2MQTT already describe their devices on the bus and because the pattern every successful local-LLM user converges on (deterministic matching first, a small tool-calling model as fallback, few exposed entities, a cache-stable prompt) is easier to enforce in a purpose-built hub than in Home Assistant, which would in exchange supply a phone app, Matter and a voice server for free. The 16 GB CPU-only laptop caps live voice at a 4B-class model (Qwen3.5-4B with thinking off under llama-server), which on comparable CPUs reaches a tool call in about one second only when its static prompt prefix is cached; the MacBook is an optional accelerator for 27B-class models and never a dependency. Speech works best with two recognisers (GigaAM v3 for Russian, Parakeet v3 for English) and Piper voices, starting as push-to-talk from an HTTPS web app. Radios split cleanly into Zigbee for bought devices, Wi-Fi for home-built ESP32 nodes and 433 MHz through the owned CC1101 stick, with Matter over Thread left out of version one even though IKEA's cheapest bulbs now require it. A first round of a EUR 16.95 Zigbee coordinator, two Zigbee bulbs, a metering plug, a stick-on button and two ESP32 boards with lux, radar and motion sensors fits under EUR 100 at the promotional prices live on 25 November 2026, with about EUR 18 of headroom for the button and postage, whose prices could not be verified. Three unknowns can still move these recommendations: the laptop's actual CPU, the Russian tool-calling accuracy of current small models (no published data exists), and whether a hub other than Home Assistant can serve ESPHome voice satellites in practice (no public reference implementation was found).

## Four routes exist, and the evidence leaves two standing

Every route to a local smart home shares the same reusable parts: ESPHome firmware, Zigbee2MQTT, an MQTT broker, the Wyoming speech services and any OpenAI-compatible model server. The routes differ in who owns the core, meaning device state, automations, the user interface and the voice server. The choice is therefore about ownership and maintenance, not about what the device edge can do.

| Route | What it delivers today | What it costs | Verdict here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home Assistant as the core | Voice pipeline, local-model agents, MCP, companion apps, Zigbee, Matter and Thread in one product; deterministic commands answered before any model is asked | Monthly breaking changes; two supported install types; cloud-touching defaults for push, Matter commissioning and remote access | Strongest alternative; declined for control of the model interface, not for footprint |
| Another established hub (openHAB, ioBroker, Domoticz, FHEM, Gladys, Node-RED) | Mature device support; openHAB 5.2 adds an LLM interpreter and an MCP server | No ready local-model voice path; openHAB lacks ESPHome voice support | No advantage over either neighbour |
| Own hub on MQTT, reusing ESPHome and Zigbee2MQTT | Self-describing devices almost for free; rules as code; a model interface built around a narrow tool set | Interface, rule engine, voice server and tool layer must be built; each extra device family needs an adapter; maintenance never ends | Chosen |
| Hybrid: Home Assistant as device and app layer, own service for voice and models | Keeps the phone app, integrations and Matter commissioning | Home Assistant's upgrade churn stays in the system | The fallback if the interface or the voice server proves too costly |
| Everything from scratch | Total control | Rebuilds work that hundreds of contributors ship every month; no account of anyone doing it | Ruled out |

**Home Assistant** (core 2026.11.3, 23 November 2026) is the most complete and most active option, and it now carries first-party hooks for local models: an Ollama integration, a llama.cpp and OpenAI-compatible integration since 2026.8, an MCP server and client, and a built-in agent that handles a command first and passes only unmatched requests to the model ([GitHub releases](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/releases); [llama.cpp integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/llama_cpp/); [AI in Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/09/11/ai-in-home-assistant)). Its footprint is small, with a stated minimum of 2 GB of RAM on a Raspberry Pi ([Installation](https://www.home-assistant.io/installation/)). Its documented costs lie elsewhere. The 2026.11 release post alone lists **at least 44 backward-incompatible changes** ([2026.11 release post](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/11/04/release-202611/)), and the container install has no apps, which leaves Thread and Z-Wave without out-of-the-box support there and pushes users towards the appliance-style operating system ([Installation](https://www.home-assistant.io/installation/)). Three convenience paths also break this household's no-cloud rule unless they are avoided: standard push notifications travel through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, unencrypted on that leg ([Companion app notification details](https://companion.home-assistant.io/docs/notifications/notification-details/)), Matter commissioning needs a phone with Google Play Services or iOS 16 ([Matter integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/matter/)), and remote access is the paid subscription's job ([Rename announcement](https://home-assistant.io/blog/2026/10/02/big-tech-ruined-the-cloud-so-were-renaming-ours/)).

The other hubs are alive but offer less for this purpose. openHAB 5.2 (July 2026) shipped an LLM interpreter and an MCP server, yet only Google Gemini was supported at launch, with OpenAI-compatible servers such as Ollama announced as planned ([openHAB 5.2 release post](https://www.openhab.org/blog/2026-07-05-openhab-5-2-release.html)), and its own forum reports no ESPHome voice support ([openHAB forum thread](https://community.openhab.org/t/revisiting-local-llm-powered-voice-assistants-in-2026/168251)). Gladys Assistant 5 has a built-in assistant, but its release post does not say whether the model runs locally ([Gladys Assistant 5 post](https://gladysassistant.com/blog/gladys-assistant-5/)). The stand-alone voice projects of the previous generation have faded: Rhasspy is archived and its components now live in the Open Home Foundation's voice organisation ([rhasspy/rhasspy](https://github.com/rhasspy/rhasspy); [OHF-Voice/wyoming](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/wyoming)), Willow's latest commits are CI maintenance ([HeyWillow/willow](https://github.com/HeyWillow/willow)), and almost every working LLM-for-the-home project, such as home-llm or the 87-tool ha-mcp server, is a layer on Home Assistant ([home-llm](https://github.com/acon96/home-llm); [ha-mcp](https://github.com/homeassistant-ai/ha-mcp)). Three lean hubs started in 2025 and 2026 on the MQTT-plus-ESPHome idea sit at 0, 3 and 9 GitHub stars, which makes them references to read and not platforms to adopt ([fdatoo/switchyard](https://github.com/fdatoo/switchyard); [gethome-inc/gethome-hub](https://github.com/gethome-inc/gethome-hub); [elaris-control/core](https://github.com/elaris-control/core)). Building the device edge from scratch competes with a firmware project whose single November 2026 release merged 1,463 pull requests from over 240 contributors ([ESPHome 2026.11 post](https://esphome.io/blog/2026/11/18/esphome-2026-11/)).

First-hand accounts of **self-built hubs** are few, mostly from 2023 to 2025, and self-selected, since people who abandon a custom hub rarely write about it; Reddit could not be read for this research. Those who stayed satisfied kept Zigbee2MQTT and ESPHome at the edge and wrote only logic; a two-year retrospective describes ESPHome on plain MQTT with Mosquitto and Zigbee2MQTT as remarkably stable, with all configuration in Git ([sambobb.com](https://www.sambobb.com/posts/home-automation-without-home-assistant/)). What they lost was the integration ecosystem and, above all, the user interface, and one developer who has maintained a custom platform since 2013 concludes that personal software is more liability than asset ([HN thread, 2023-06-05](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36191688)). The best counter-argument comes from a Home Assistant user: the product's value is being the single place that connects whatever device arrives next ([HN comment, 2026-03-17](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47406771)). The hybrid route recurs in these accounts, most recently in a custom voice pipeline that uses Home Assistant only through its REST API for device control ([bitworking.org](https://bitworking.org/news/2026/05/surprising-things-i-learned-putting-together-a-home-brain/)). No account was found of a custom hub that carries local-LLM voice control without Home Assistant, so this household's design has no direct precedent.

What tips the balance is the experience of people who run local LLM voice on Home Assistant. Their response times cluster at **1 to 2 seconds on 24 GB GPUs** and about 3 seconds on 8 GB ([HA forum: journey](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860)), at 10 to 21 seconds on a fanless Intel N150 ([HA forum: N150 journey](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/running-a-local-llm-on-an-intel-n150-mini-pc-for-home-assistant-a-practical-journey/1007724)), and at about 0.05 seconds for commands that bypass the model ([HA forum: AI Plugin](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/integration-tuned-for-running-assist-on-a-local-llm-in-8-gb-vram/1025725)). Nearly every account converges on one shape: deterministic matching first, a small tool-calling model second, few exposed entities (one user was fine with 32, another saw wrong-device actions with 53), prompt caching and no reasoning mode. Home Assistant users reach that shape by restricting exposure and adding sentence triggers, after months of tuning that the most experienced author would not recommend to an average user ([HA forum: journey](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860)). A purpose-built hub enforces it by design. That, and not footprint, is the argument for the custom route. The argument against it is breadth over time: every device family beyond the self-describing ones costs adapter work that Home Assistant users get from monthly releases. The decision stays reversible, because devices that announce themselves over MQTT can attach to Home Assistant later or run beside it.

## Self-describing devices on MQTT shrink the hub to five build items

The recommended stack has eight layers, and the rest of this report justifies each choice.

| Layer | Choice | Built or reused | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Device edge | ESPHome on ESP32 nodes over Wi-Fi and MQTT; bought Zigbee devices through Zigbee2MQTT; 433 MHz and infrared through the M5 stick | Reused | Breaking changes in ESPHome and in the discovery schema |
| Bus | Mosquitto with one credential per device | Reused | Single point of failure |
| Hub core | Python: discovery consumers, registry, state, scheduler, rule engine, safety policy | Built | Open-ended maintenance |
| Intelligence | Deterministic matcher, then Qwen3.5-4B on llama-server, then a 27B-class model on the Mac | Built on reused runtimes | Laptop CPU unknown; Russian tool calling unmeasured |
| Speech | GigaAM v3 and Parakeet v3 through onnx-asr, Silero VAD, Piper | Reused | No timing on a laptop-class CPU |
| Voice endpoints | Push-to-talk in the web app, then the M5 devices, Android phones and ESP32 satellites over the ESPHome native API | Server side built | No public reference for a voice server outside Home Assistant |
| App | Web app served by the hub over HTTPS with a private certificate authority | Built | iOS background limits |
| Host and network | The Linux laptop around the clock; an own OpenWrt router with an IoT zone | Owned and bought | Lid, sleep, battery and disk-unlock settings |

A custom hub is feasible because self-description already travels on the bus without Home Assistant. Zigbee2MQTT puts **5,868 devices from 616 vendors** behind one retained JSON document, `bridge/devices`, whose `exposes` entries carry types, value ranges, units and access bits ([Supported devices](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/supported-devices/); [Exposes](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/guide/usage/exposes.html)). ESPHome publishes Home Assistant-style MQTT discovery by default, a format that 36 tools emit and that openHAB and Domoticz already consume ([MQTT component](https://esphome.io/components/mqtt/); [MQTT integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/mqtt/)). Two consumers therefore cover most of the edge, and the hub's unavoidable build list reduces to five items: the discovery consumers, a rule and schedule engine, a phone-usable interface, the server side of a voice satellite protocol, and the tool layer between the model and the bus. Builders report pain in the third, ESPHome specifies the fourth only as streaming to Home Assistant, and no small reusable component was found for either ([HN thread, 2023-06-05](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36191688); [ESPHome voice assistant](https://esphome.io/components/voice_assistant/)). The discovery schema keeps changing with Home Assistant releases (the MQTT `object_id` option was removed in 2026.4), so the consumer should read the stable core of config topic, device block, state and command topics, and tolerate unknown keys ([2026.4 release post](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/04/01/release-20264/)). Homie v5, the cleaner self-description convention on paper, has too little adoption to target: its repository's latest release object still dates from 2019 ([homieiot/convention](https://github.com/homieiot/convention)).

The broker is Mosquitto, which in version 2.1 gained a built-in WebSocket implementation and a SQLite persistence plugin ([Mosquitto 2.1.0 announcement](https://mosquitto.org/blog/2026/01/version-2-1-0-released/)); EMQX moved to a Business Source License and no longer qualifies as open source ([EMQX licence FAQ](https://www.emqx.com/en/content/license-faq)). ESPHome's own documentation names the broker as the single point of failure of an MQTT design, which is the cost of this bus ([Native API component](https://esphome.io/components/api/)). Running ESPHome without Home Assistant needs one setting: remove the `api:` block or set its `reboot_timeout` to `0s`, otherwise the node reboots every 15 minutes while it waits for a client ([MQTT component](https://esphome.io/components/mqtt/)). The features that make an ESPHome node more than a sensor (voice assistant, Bluetooth proxy, infrared and RF proxy) exist only on the native API, which the MIT-licensed aioesphomeapi library opens to any Python program ([ESPHome integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/esphome/); [aioesphomeapi client.py](https://github.com/esphome/aioesphomeapi/blob/main/aioesphomeapi/client.py)). The workable split is MQTT for ordinary entities and the native API only on voice nodes; that hybrid follows from the documentation but was not found described as a tested setup.

Other firmware families need small adapters. Tasmota announces itself on its own `tasmota/discovery` tree, having removed legacy Home Assistant discovery ([Tasmota Home Assistant page](https://tasmota.github.io/docs/Home-Assistant/)). Shelly's stock firmware has the richest local interface but speaks JSON-RPC over MQTT with no discovery ([Shelly MQTT component](https://shelly-api-docs.shelly.cloud/gen2/ComponentsAndServices/Mqtt/)). WLED publishes a fixed topic scheme ([WLED MQTT docs](https://kno.wled.ge/interfaces/mqtt/)). OpenMQTTGateway, which bridges Bluetooth LE, 433 MHz, infrared and LoRa to MQTT with discovery on by default, fills the radio gaps ([OpenMQTTGateway docs](https://docs.openmqttgateway.com/)).

### Zigbee for bought devices, Wi-Fi for built nodes, Thread deferred

No single radio fits every role, and the evidence supports a deliberate split.

| Radio | Role in this home | Path onto the bus | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zigbee 3.0 | Bought bulbs, plugs, buttons, battery sensors, the curtain drive | Zigbee2MQTT | Core, from round one |
| Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz | USB-powered ESP32 nodes, LED controllers, voice satellites | ESPHome over MQTT; native API for voice | Core, from round one |
| 433 MHz sub-GHz | Fixed-code remote sockets, weather sensors, RF blind motors | ESPHome `cc1101` on the owned M5 stick, or OpenMQTTGateway | When a device needs it |
| Bluetooth LE | BTHome sensors and buttons, SwitchBot curtain drives | OpenMQTTGateway on an ESP32 | Optional, per device |
| ESP-NOW | Home-built battery nodes | An always-on ESP32 gateway | Later, only for sensors that cannot be bought |
| LoRa and Meshtastic | A sensor out of Wi-Fi and Zigbee reach | Meshtastic JSON over MQTT from the Cardputer | Niche |
| Thread and Matter | IKEA's 2025 to 2026 range | Border router plus the standalone Matter Server | Deferred |
| Z-Wave, Bluetooth Mesh | None | No ESP32 radio for Z-Wave; no open-hub path found for Bluetooth Mesh | Skip |

Zigbee wins for anything bought because the catalogue is the largest and coin-cell life is measured in years: SONOFF rates its SNZB-02P at about four years on a CR2477 ([SONOFF SNZB-02P](https://sonoff.tech/en-us/products/sonoff-zigbee-temperature-and-humidity-sensor-snzb-02p)). Building battery sensors on ESP32 does not compete. Documented ESP32-C6 and ESP32-H2 Zigbee or Thread builds drained in **about two months** ([DFRobot forum](https://www.dfrobot.com/forum/topic/401992); [Home Assistant community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/esp32-h2-thread-with-deep-sleep-for-cheap-esp32-battery-sensors/919132)), and a Wi-Fi node needs 3.9 seconds of radio time per wake against roughly 200 milliseconds for ESP-NOW ([ThingPulse](https://thingpulse.com/esp32-ultra-long-battery-life-with-espnow/); [ThingPulse ePulse Feather](https://thingpulse.com/epulse-feather-espnow-a-dream-team/)). The rule that follows is to buy battery devices and build only powered ones. Bluetooth LE stays an optional extra: BTHome is an open broadcast format for sensors and buttons that an ESP32 gateway relays to MQTT ([BTHome](https://bthome.io/)). That rule also keeps the Wi-Fi client count low, which matters on consumer routers: one ASUS user saw clients disconnect once 15 ESP32 devices had joined, and TP-Link puts the hard limit of one of its routers at 64 clients per band ([ASUS ROG forum](https://rog-forum.asus.com/t5/gaming-routers/wi-fi-clients-disconnect-when-15-esp32-devices-are-connected-gt/td-p/1110167); [TP-Link community](https://community.tp-link.com/en/home/forum/topic/271106)). For built nodes the ESP32-C6 is the all-rounder, the ESP32-S3 with PSRAM is the voice and display chip, and the ESP32-C3 is the cheapest Wi-Fi node ([DroneBot Workshop ESP32 guide 2026](https://dronebotworkshop.com/esp32-2026/)), with the caveat that cheap C3 SuperMini clones have recurring Wi-Fi handshake and range problems ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/esp32-c3-super-mini-wi-fi-auth-expired-no-tx-current-spikes-wroom-32d-works-perfectly/1021889)).

The coordinator is a budget question with a clean upgrade path. Zigbee2MQTT prefers a wired Ethernet adapter over Wi-Fi-attached ones, and reviewers favour LAN coordinators because they sit centrally regardless of where the server is ([Zigbee2MQTT adapters](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/guide/adapters/); [SmartHomeScene top picks](https://smarthomescene.com/top-picks/best-zigbee-coordinators-for-home-assistant/)). On a stationary Linux laptop a USB stick on an extension lead is enough to start: the SMLIGHT SLZB-07 costs **EUR 16.95** at an EU maker shop, against EUR 39.95 for the Ethernet SLZB-06M ([hobbyelectronica: SLZB-07](https://www.hobbyelectronica.nl/product/slzb-07-zigbee-coordinator-usb/); [hobbyelectronica: Zigbee en Matter](https://www.hobbyelectronica.nl/product-categorie/platformen/zigbee-matter/)). Both appear on Zigbee2MQTT's EmberZNet list, one of the two driver families that support coordinator backup, which matters because switching adapters can otherwise mean re-pairing every device ([Zigbee2MQTT EmberZNet adapters](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/guide/adapters/emberznet.html); [Zigbee2MQTT adapters](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/guide/adapters/)).

Thread and Matter are the one popular option to leave out of version one. IKEA Germany's smart bulb, plug, remote and sensor listings are now all Matter products, from EUR 4.99 per bulb, and the product pages state that Matter over Thread needs a Thread border router ([IKEA DE: Smart Home LED-Leuchtmittel](https://www.ikea.com/de/de/cat/smart-home-led-leuchtmittel-36813/); [IKEA DE: GRILLPLATS](https://www.ikea.com/de/de/p/grillplats-steckdose-smart-60604238/)). Zigbee2MQTT does not support multiprotocol firmware, so Thread means a second radio ([Zigbee2MQTT EmberZNet adapters](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/guide/adapters/emberznet.html)), and the range drew connection complaints through 2026 ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/05/15/ikea-matter-over-thread-review-amazing-smart-home-tech-when-they-work)). The standalone Matter Server with its WebSocket API is the right building block when Thread is wanted later ([matterjs-server README](https://github.com/matter-js/matterjs-server)), but the one detailed 2026 account of running Thread outside Home Assistant still leaned on Home Assistant and a phone ecosystem for commissioning ([openHAB forum, 2026-05-23](https://community.openhab.org/t/how-to-use-an-orange-pi-zero-2-running-otbr-with-openhab/169352)). Nothing in this household's feature list requires it.

### The two M5 devices earn their place through sub-GHz radio and built-in controls

Both owned devices duplicate ordinary Wi-Fi and Bluetooth roles; their unique value lies in their radios and their human interface. The "Cardputer mesh version" matches the **Cardputer Mesh Kit**, a Cardputer-Adv (ESP32-S3, keyboard, screen, codec with microphone and 1 W speaker, infrared emitter) with an SX1262 LoRa and GNSS cap and Meshtastic preinstalled ([M5Stack docs](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/core/Cardputer_Mesh_Kit); [M5Stack Cardputer-Adv docs](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/core/Cardputer-Adv)). It suits a handheld control panel, an admin terminal and a push-to-talk prototype with spoken replies. Its LoRa cap covers 868 to 923 MHz only, and Meshtastic's JSON-over-MQTT gateway works on ESP32 nodes with a private broker, so LoRa stays available for a sensor beyond Wi-Fi and Zigbee reach, which an apartment rarely has ([Meshtastic MQTT docs](https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/integrations/mqtt/)). The unit draws 120 to 155 mA, so permanent duty means USB power ([M5Stack Cardputer-Adv docs](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/core/Cardputer-Adv)).

The stick is most probably an M5StickC Plus2 with a third-party CC1101 hat of the kind sold for Bruce firmware, and less likely the 2026 StickS3 ([Bruce firmware](https://github.com/BruceDevices/firmware)). The difference matters: the Plus2 has only a buzzer and an infrared transmitter, while the StickS3 adds a real speaker, 8 MB of PSRAM and an infrared receiver ([M5Stack docs](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/core/M5StickC%20PLUS2); [CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/01/23/m5stack-sticks3-esp32-s3-mini-iot-dev-kit-features-1-14-inch-color-display-speaker-and-mic-for-voice-interaction/)). Tethered to USB, the stick becomes the home's 433 MHz and infrared gateway through ESPHome's native `cc1101` component with `remote_receiver` and `remote_transmitter` ([ESPHome CC1101](https://esphome.io/components/cc1101.html)), and its microphone can publish a sound level. Two limits apply. Fixed-code remotes are the easy case, while forum users report that rolling-code remotes could not be captured with comparable bridges ([Home Assistant thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/433mhz-kl180-remote-programming/1005908)). And the research found no ESPHome device page or tested configuration for the Cardputer, the StickC Plus2 or the StickS3; the nearest is a community configuration for the original M5StickC ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/working-esphome-yaml-for-m5stickc/538050)). Bringing both devices up under ESPHome is a spike to run before anything relies on them.

### The wake-up rule needs a lux sensor, a radar and a sound level, not a model

The household's signature trigger (morning, a certain illuminance, the sound of people waking) is ordinary sensor logic once the values arrive as MQTT state. Every part has a cheap module with native ESPHome support: a BH1750 or VEML7700 for illuminance at $1 to $3, an HLK-LD2410 radar that separates still from moving targets at $3 to $6, and an AM312 PIR for instant motion ([atomic14 sensor index](https://www.atomic14.com/esp32/sensors/); [atomic14 presence sensors](https://www.atomic14.com/esp32/sensors/presence-sensors/)). ESPHome's `sound_level` sensor reduces a microphone to relative decibel values on the device ([ESPHome sound_level](https://esphome.io/components/sensor/sound_level.html)); the values are relative to the microphone's full scale, so thresholds need tuning per node. Recognising the kind of sound is beyond ESPHome and should not go to an audio LLM. In a 2026 benchmark of 2,242 clips, dedicated audio taggers and embedding models matched or exceeded the best LLM at category level, and wrong LLM answers were stated confidently 92 to 100% of the time ([arXiv 2608.02397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02397)). A small tagger on the hub is the upgrade if level and presence prove too coarse: sherpa-onnx ships a 26 MB model that runs at about 3% of real time on one CPU thread ([sherpa-onnx audio tagging models](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/audio-tagging/pretrained_models.html)). The node then publishes levels or labels only, never audio.

## A 4B model on the laptop reaches a tool call in about a second once its prompt is cached

The always-on host sets the ceiling. Without a GPU, 4B-class models generate at 8.5 to 16.8 tokens per second on the server and laptop CPUs measured ([igllama showcase](https://bkataru.github.io/igllama/showcase/qwen35-small/); [LiteRT Gemma 4 E4B](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E4B-it-litert-lm)), and dense 8 to 9B models fall to 3 to 6.5, too slow for live voice ([igllama showcase](https://bkataru.github.io/igllama/showcase/qwen35-small/); [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/testing-local-llms-without-gpu)). Generation is not the binding constraint; prompt processing is. A 4B-class model reads prompts at about 170 to 195 tokens per second on a recent CPU ([LiteRT Gemma 4 E4B](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E4B-it-litert-lm); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)), so a 1,500 to 2,000-token prompt of instructions, tool schemas and entity names costs **8 to 12 seconds cold** by simple division. With a 500-token prefix held in llama-server's prompt cache, the one CPU-only measurement found shows the tool call ready in a median 0.87 to 1.15 seconds and complete end to end in 2.1 to 3.6 seconds, against 6.4 seconds before any output on the first, uncached request ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)). The design consequence is a byte-stable prefix (instructions, tools, the entity catalogue in fixed order) followed by a short volatile tail (the time, the few relevant states, the utterance), pinned to one slot and warmed at service start.

Two caveats bound these numbers. The laptop's CPU is unknown, so every figure is an analogy until `llama-bench` and a real prompt run on the machine; an older Core i5 laptop evaluated prompts at only about 20 tokens per second and generated at 7 to 10 with 2B to 4B models ([It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/testing-local-llms-without-gpu)), in which case the fallback is a 2B-class model such as Qwen3.5-2B at 1.28 GB ([Qwen3.5-2B GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-2B-GGUF)). And "no dedicated GPU" does not mean no acceleration: on a Lunar Lake laptop the integrated GPU read prompts seven times faster than the CPU ([LiteRT Gemma 4 E4B](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E4B-it-litert-lm)), so the integrated GPU deserves a test before settling on CPU-only inference.

| Tier | Where | Starting choice | Job | Evidence and caveats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fast path | Laptop, inside the hub | Deterministic matcher over the device registry, in English and Russian | Frequent, unambiguous commands in milliseconds | Bypassed commands answer in about 0.05 s on Home Assistant setups |
| Small model | Laptop, llama-server | Qwen3.5-4B at Q4_K_M (2.74 GB), thinking off; test Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B, GigaChat3.1 Lightning (6.47 GB) and LFM3-1.7B (1.10 GB) against it | Commands the matcher misses; simple state questions | 97.5% on an independent tool test; weak restraint score; no Russian tool data |
| Large model | MacBook, when reachable | Qwen3.8-27B or Gemma 4 31B (17.5 GB at 4-bit); T-pro-it-2.1 for Russian | Rule drafting, multi-step and ambiguous requests, later camera snapshots | 42% against 69 to 77% on Google's agent benchmark between the 4B and 12 to 31B sizes; no measured M5 Pro speeds |

Qwen3.5-4B earns the first slot because it scored **97.5% on an independent 40-case, eight-tool test** of 13 models, ahead of 20B and 24B models ([J.D. Hodges](https://www.jdhodges.com/blog/local-llms-on-tool-calling-2026-pt1-local-lm/)), and its 4-bit file is 2.74 GB ([Qwen3.5-4B GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-4B-GGUF)). It has two faults to engineer around. It thinks by default, and in the one measurement available thinking raised the median CPU tool call from between 2.1 and 3.6 seconds to 13.1 seconds, so thinking must be switched off explicitly ([Qwen3.5-9B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)). And it is poor at declining: on NVIDIA's When2Call table it scores 37.8 against 62.9 for Gemma 4 E4B and 63.4 for the Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B post-train ([NVIDIA Research](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-E4B-4GB/)). In a home, wrongly firing a device costs more than declining, which is why the hub validates every call in code and why Bolt E4B belongs in the head-to-head test. Quantisation matters as much as the model: one user replayed 33 real requests and got 9 empty answers at a 3-bit quantisation and none at 4-bit ([HA forum: AI Plugin](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/integration-tuned-for-running-assist-on-a-local-llm-in-8-gb-vram/1025725)). The other two candidates are there for specific reasons: LFM3-1.7B is the smallest model that lists Russian, with vendor-only benchmarks and a vendor-specific licence ([Liquid AI blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3); [LFM3-1.7B GGUF files](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B-GGUF)), and GigaChat3.1 Lightning is the Russian-first option discussed below.

For the runtime, **llama-server** fits this host better than Ollama. It combines prompt caching on by default, slot save and restore, separate thread counts for generation and prompt processing, JSON-schema and GBNF constraints, a multi-model router and a health endpoint in one MIT-licensed binary ([llama-server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)). Ollama is easier to run but defaults to a 4,096-token context and unloads models after five minutes, both wrong for a home agent unless changed ([Ollama FAQ](https://docs.ollama.com/faq)). On the MacBook an MLX-backed runtime is likely fastest, though no trustworthy M5 Pro measurement exists and published estimates disagree by a factor of two; the reference `mlx_lm.server` is explicitly not recommended for production ([mlx-lm SERVER.md](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/SERVER.md)). The Mac earns its tier through quality, not speed: Google's own agent benchmark puts Gemma 4 E4B at 42.2% against 69.0% for the 12B and 76.9% for the 31B model ([Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/); [Gemma 4 overview](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/)). The candidates there are Gemma 4 31B and Qwen3.8-27B, the newest open 27B-class Qwen, both under Apache 2.0 ([Qwen3.8-27B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)). Both tiers should expose the same OpenAI-compatible interface and tool schemas, so that failover is an ordered list of endpoints with short timeouts, and simple device commands stay on the laptop even when the Mac is home.

Cameras, which the owner has deferred, belong to the Mac tier when they come: a conventional detector triggers a snapshot and a 4 to 9B vision model describes it, a job too slow for CPU-class hardware, where a 4B vision model took about 18 seconds per request on an N150 ([HA forum: N150 journey](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/running-a-local-llm-on-an-intel-n150-mini-pc-for-home-assistant-a-practical-journey/1007724)). A dedicated AI computer is not justified before the laptop is measured. An N150 mini PC at a median $349 would be slower than most laptops, and the cheapest step that changes the experience is fast unified memory, such as a Mac mini M6 at $899 and 4 W idle ([ComputingForGeeks price tracker](https://computingforgeeks.com/mini-pc-prices/); [Macworld](https://macworld.com/article/3220063/apple-launches-new-m6-mac-mini-with-another-price-hike.html); [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/103253)).

### Deterministic matching comes first and the model only proposes

The agent design that the evidence supports is plain. In the largest 2026 study of models under 10B, run on financial-text tasks, routing-based multi-agent systems completed 72% of tasks against 80% for a single agent while using 1.8 times the tokens ([arXiv 2604.19299](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19299v1)). A swarm of small chatty agents is therefore the least supported option. A deterministic router in front of a few narrow prompts (device command, state question, rule drafting) keeps each prompt and tool list short, which is what small models need. Scaffolding in code delivers the measurable gains: validation, rescue parsing, retry nudges and step enforcement raised an 8B model from about 53% to between 82 and 86.5% on one agent harness ([DEV Community write-up of Forge](https://dev.to/monuminu/llm-agent-guardrails-the-engineering-playbook-for-taking-an-8b-local-model-from-53-to-99-on-18c)). Grammar constraints belong late and narrow. On models of 1.7B and below, forcing the whole answer into a schema raised validity to 100% while cutting accuracy from 19.7% to 11.0% ([arXiv 2605.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)), so the constraint should cover the tool name and enumerated entity identifiers, with dates, units and percentages resolved in code. MCP is a reasonable internal tool contract after its stateless 2026-07-28 revision ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)), but small models score low on MCP benchmarks (41.37 for Qwen3.5-4B in one vendor table), so the model should see a per-request subset and never the catalogue ([Liquid AI blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)).

Safety follows the same architecture. A poisoned calendar invite made Gemini's home agent open windows and switch on a boiler in 2025 ([arXiv 2508.12175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12175)), and the published defences are structural: the action-selector pattern restricts the model to mapping a request onto predefined tool calls ([arXiv 2506.08837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08837)). Applied here, the model selects among pre-registered entity identifiers and bounded values, the hub re-validates, and an injected instruction cannot create a capability. Device names, sensor text and transcribed background speech are untrusted data. Actions with physical consequences (a curtain motor, a heater on a plug, anything at night) need confirmation or stay outside the model's allow-list, and a model-drafted rule is a delayed action that the owner approves before it runs. Manual control, schedules and rules must work with every model stopped. No study was found that tests injection through device names or sensor text in a local home agent, so this part rests on general patterns applied to the setting.

### Russian costs 5 to 12 points of tool-calling accuracy

The bilingual requirement is where the evidence is thinnest. The only published Russian-versus-English function-calling pairs come from T-Tech's model cards, and they show the same model scoring lower in Russian by about 5 to 12 points: 56.5 against 62.2 for T-lite-it-2.1, 52.6 against 59.4 for Qwen3-8B, 57.3 against 69.2 for Qwen3-32B ([T-lite-it-2.1 model card](https://huggingface.co/t-tech/T-lite-it-2.1); [T-pro-it-2.1 model card](https://huggingface.co/t-tech/T-pro-it-2.1)). **No Russian tool-calling result exists for Qwen3.5, Gemma 4, Nemotron Bolt or LFM3**, which only claim the language. The best-documented Russian tool callers are dense 8B and 33B fine-tunes of Qwen3 under Apache 2.0, T-lite-it-2.1 and T-pro-it-2.1; the first is too slow for live voice on this CPU, the second belongs on the Mac. The only Russian-first model whose architecture suits the laptop is Sber's MIT-licensed GigaChat3.1-10B-A1.8B, a mixture-of-experts model with 1.8B active parameters and a 6.47 GB 4-bit file, which leads on Russian knowledge tests but publishes no tool-calling score and no CPU speed ([GigaChat3.1-10B-A1.8B model card](https://huggingface.co/ai-sage/GigaChat3.1-10B-A1.8B); [GigaChat3.1 GGUF repository](https://huggingface.co/ai-sage/GigaChat3.1-10B-A1.8B-GGUF)).

The practical answer is a private regression suite in both languages, run against every candidate on each tier and after every model or runtime update, with unsafe executions and missed commands counted separately. Headline accuracy flatters: a stub that never acts scored 77% on a community Home Assistant benchmark ([Selora blog](https://selorahomes.com/blog/selora-ai-llm-v0.6.1/)). Two design rules reduce the language risk whatever the model. Every device gets a stable ASCII identifier with Russian and English names and aliases attached, so the model never has to reproduce Cyrillic in a tool call and the matcher handles Russian case endings through alias lists. And spoken confirmations come from templates in code, which keeps the wording consistent and avoids slow Russian generation on a CPU; the authors of a Russian-adapted tokenizer report Russian text generation up to twice as fast as with the stock one, a measure of what general tokenizers cost ([RuadaptQwen3-4B-Instruct model card](https://huggingface.co/RefalMachine/RuadaptQwen3-4B-Instruct)).

### Two speech recognisers beat one multilingual model

No single open model is best for both languages on a CPU, so the pipeline uses a specialist per language.

| Stage | Starting choice | Why | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wake word | None at first (push-to-talk); later microWakeWord on the satellite, one wake word per language | Runs on the device; two wake words select the language pipeline | openWakeWord on the hub (English-only training) |
| Endpointing | Silero VAD on the hub | MIT licence; under 1 ms per audio chunk on one thread | The satellite's own voice-activity model |
| Russian speech-to-text | GigaAM v3 through onnx-asr | 3.2% word error rate on short commands; 43 to 59 times real time on a desktop CPU, depending on the variant | Whisper-Podlodka-Turbo |
| English speech-to-text | Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 through onnx-asr | 33 to 35 times real time, against 3 to 5 for Whisper large-v3-turbo | Whisper large-v3-turbo as a one-model fallback |
| Text-to-speech | Piper with one ru_RU and one en_US voice | Fast on CPU; four Russian voices | Silero v5 for Russian stress and homographs (non-commercial licence) |
| Satellite protocol | ESPHome native API, served by the hub through aioesphomeapi | One implementation covers ESP32, Android and Linux satellites | xiaozhi-esp32 firmware with a self-hosted server |

Russian decides the recogniser. On independent Russian test sets GigaAM v3 averages a 7.19% word error rate and 3.2% on short commands ([alphacephei, ASR details 2026](https://alphacephei.com/nsh/2026/05/24/asr-details.html)), while the multilingual candidates fail exactly on command-style speech: in the same author's 2025 run Parakeet TDT v3 scored 19.5% and Whisper large-v3 18.6% on short Russian commands, against 1.9% for GigaAM v2 ([alphacephei, Russian models 2025](https://alphacephei.com/nsh/2025/04/18/russian-models.html)). Speed points the same way. Through ONNX Runtime on a desktop Ryzen CPU, GigaAM v3 runs at 43 to 59 times real time depending on the variant, Parakeet v3 at 33 to 35 and Whisper large-v3-turbo at 2.9 to 5.4 ([onnx-asr benchmarks](https://istupakov.github.io/onnx-asr/benchmarks/)); both specialists stay loaded comfortably in 16 GB, and the MIT-licensed onnx-asr library runs both from Python ([onnx-asr](https://github.com/istupakov/onnx-asr)). Whisper remains the one-model fallback, preferably in the Podlodka fine-tune for Russian and English ([whisper-podlodka-turbo](https://huggingface.co/bond005/whisper-podlodka-turbo)). No model is documented as handling a switch of language inside one utterance, and NVIDIA says Parakeet v3 is not fully tuned for it ([parakeet v3 discussion #1](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3/discussions/1)), so the hub should hold Cyrillic aliases for English device names. The language is selected by wake word once satellites exist, and before that by decoding each utterance with both models and keeping the transcript that matches an intent.

For output, Piper is the practical baseline with four Russian voices ([piper1-gpl](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl); [piper-voices ru_RU](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/tree/main/ru/ru_RU)). Russian stress is its weak point, and Silero v5 handles stress and homographs automatically under a non-commercial licence ([silero-models](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-models)). Streaming synthesis matters more than the voice model for perceived speed: it cut time to first audio from 5.31 to 0.56 seconds in Home Assistant's test ([AI in Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/09/11/ai-in-home-assistant)). No end-to-end measurement of this pipeline on a laptop CPU was found. The one CPU-only reference, with Whisper, a 4B model and Piper, took 4.1 to 4.6 seconds per voice turn, most of it in the language model ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)), which is one more reason to keep routine commands on the deterministic path.

Voice input should arrive in stages that match the risk. Push-to-talk from the web app needs only a secure context and works on both phone platforms ([MDN getUserMedia](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia)). The Cardputer adds a push-to-talk device with a speaker; the StickC Plus2 has a microphone but only a buzzer. Always-listening is in practice Android and ESP32 territory, because iOS does not let an app start recording from the background ([Apple Developer Forums 674632](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/674632)). Wake words then run on the device with microWakeWord, and a satellite can carry two wake words with separate pipelines, which selects the language ([ESPHome micro_wake_word](https://esphome.io/components/micro_wake_word/); [Voice Chapter 11](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/10/22/voice-chapter-11/)). Training a custom wake word is officially very difficult and openWakeWord's trainer is English-only, so a stock wake word comes first ([OHF-Voice/micro-wake-word](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/micro-wake-word); [openWakeWord](https://github.com/dscripka/openWakeWord)). Endpointing on the hub uses Silero VAD, which processes an audio chunk in under a millisecond ([Silero VAD](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad)).

The satellite protocol is now the ESPHome native API. The Wyoming satellite was archived in January 2026 in favour of it ([wyoming-satellite](https://github.com/rhasspy/wyoming-satellite)), aioesphomeapi exposes every voice call, and Home Assistant's own satellite module documents the event sequence ([aioesphomeapi client.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/esphome/aioesphomeapi/main/aioesphomeapi/client.py); [Home Assistant esphome/assist_satellite.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/core/dev/homeassistant/components/esphome/assist_satellite.py)). Because the Android app Ava and the Linux voice assistant present themselves as ESPHome devices, one hub-side implementation serves spare phones, Linux boxes and ESP32 satellites ([Ava](https://github.com/brownard/Ava); [linux-voice-assistant](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/linux-voice-assistant)). The risk is real, though. No standalone example of a voice server on this API outside Home Assistant was found, openHAB with far more contributors has not built one, and whether Ava works against another server is unconfirmed. The fallback is the MIT-licensed xiaozhi-esp32 firmware, which speaks WebSocket or MQTT plus UDP to self-hostable servers and supports M5Stack boards ([xiaozhi-esp32](https://github.com/78/xiaozhi-esp32)). Dedicated hardware waits until a spare phone has shown where far-field pickup matters; the open reference satellite, Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition, costs EUR 59 ([Home Assistant Voice PE](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice-pe/)).

## An HTTPS web app covers control and push-to-talk, but iPhones cannot be alerted without Apple

A web app served by the hub is the only client that needs no store, no code signing and no developer account on either phone platform, and in late 2026 it covers the control interface, push-to-talk capture and screen wake lock. It falls short where a smart home wants background behaviour. iOS offers no background sync, WebSockets die or hang after backgrounding, and an iPadOS 26 regression closed plain `ws://` connections from installed web apps about a second after they opened ([Apple Developer Forums thread 811063](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811063)). That bug and the browser rules point one way: serve `https://` and `wss://` from day one, with aggressive reconnects and an HTTP fallback, since microphone access, service workers and wake lock all require a secure context ([MDN, features restricted to secure contexts](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Secure_Contexts/features_restricted_to_secure_contexts)).

The certificate route consistent with "no cloud, no recurring cost" is a **private certificate authority** such as Caddy's internal one. It works fully offline, at the price of installing and explicitly trusting the root on every phone, a two-step procedure on iOS ([Caddy, Automatic HTTPS](https://caddyserver.com/docs/automatic-https); [Apple Support 102390](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102390)), and leaf certificates must stay at 825 days or less for iOS ([Apple Support 103769](https://support.apple.com/en-us/103769)). Android apps ignore user-installed roots unless they opt in, so a later Android shell has to bundle the root ([Android network security configuration](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/security-config)). A public domain with Let's Encrypt DNS-01 certificates needs no client setup but adds a registrar, a DNS credential stored on the hub and internet-dependent renewals on a schedule shrinking to 45 days by 2028 ([Let's Encrypt challenge types](https://letsencrypt.org/docs/challenge-types/); [Let's Encrypt, From 90 to 45](https://letsencrypt.org/2025/12/02/from-90-to-45)). The hub should answer on a unicast DNS name under `home.arpa`, the domain reserved for home networks, and not on `.local`, because Android resolves mDNS names inconsistently and not at all over a VPN ([RFC 8375](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8375); [Android DNS Resolver docs](https://source.android.com/docs/core/ota/modular-system/dns-resolver)). Whether an iPhone keeps the microphone permission across launches of an installed web app, and whether service workers behave on a user-trusted root, is not settled by any 2025 or 2026 source and needs a test on the household's own phones.

Notifications are the hard limit. On Android a fully local design is straightforward: a small app holds a WebSocket to the hub in a foreground service, as ntfy's instant delivery does ([ntfy phone docs](https://docs.ntfy.sh/subscribe/phone/)), and installing one's own app over ADB is untouched by Google's developer-verification programme, whose global rollout begins in January 2027 ([Google developer-verification FAQ](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq); [Android Developers Blog, 19 Nov 2026](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/android-developer-verification-2027)). On iOS, every way of alerting a device whose app is closed passes through Apple. Web Push from the hub works for web apps added to the Home Screen with no developer account, the payload is encrypted, and Apple learns only that an event happened and when; the hub then needs outbound internet access ([WebKit blog](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/); [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/sending-web-push-notifications-in-web-apps-and-browsers)). The local alternative, Local Push Connectivity, needs an entitlement that Apple must grant ([Apple, Local push connectivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/networkextension/local-push-connectivity)), and free native builds expire after seven days ([Apple, Choosing a Membership](https://developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships/)). The honest position for a strictly local system is in-app alerts on iPhones while the app is open, Android phones or wall panels for anything urgent, and encrypted Web Push as a named, optional exception.

Remote access belongs to a later phase and faces a similar trade. In a rented flat no port can be forwarded, so the choice is an outbound tunnel through Tailscale's free plan, which makes a company's control plane a dependency, or a rented relay server, which is a subscription in all but name ([Tailscale pricing](https://tailscale.com/pricing); [Hetzner community tutorial](https://community.hetzner.com/tutorials/bypass-cgnat-with-a-wireguard-vps-relay/)).

### A second router and four laptop settings make the host dependable

Because the building's router cannot be assumed configurable, segmentation lives on a user-owned OpenWrt-capable router in routing mode behind it. It carries a trusted zone for phones and the hub and an IoT zone with no forwarding to the internet, with rules that let the hub reach devices and devices reach only the broker. OpenWrt's default firewall defines forwarding between zones only from the trusted zone to the internet, so an added IoT zone has no internet path unless one is created ([OpenWrt firewall4 default configuration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openwrt/firewall4/master/root/etc/config/firewall)). OpenWrt's source tree carries build targets for inexpensive candidates such as the Cudy WR3000 and TR3000 and the GL.iNet GL-MT3000 ([OpenWrt source, filogic.mk](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openwrt/openwrt/main/target/linux/mediatek/image/filogic.mk)), though Cudy warns that units with a newer flash chip need OpenWrt 24.10.5 or later ([Cudy, OpenWrt software download](https://www.cudy.com/blogs/faq/openwrt-software-download)). The resulting double NAT is harmless for outbound traffic and shields the flat from the building network. mDNS does not cross zones, so devices should dial a fixed hub name and the hub should use static leases ([ESPHome FAQ](https://esphome.io/guides/faq/)); devices without internet also need time from a local server. On the bus, Mosquitto requires authentication to be configured explicitly, and the baseline is one credential per device with topic access control ([Mosquitto authentication methods](https://mosquitto.org/documentation/authentication-methods/)). ESPHome has moved the same way: API encryption has been mandatory since 2026.1 and firmware updates have been encrypted since 2026.9 ([ESPHome 2026.1.0](https://esphome.io/blog/2026/01/21/esphome-2026-1/); [ESPHome 2026.9.0](https://esphome.io/blog/2026/09/16/esphome-2026-9/)). The router is a second-round purchase, and none of this blocks the first slice.

A laptop works as a round-the-clock server, with its battery as a built-in UPS, once four things are set deliberately. systemd-logind must ignore the lid switch and sleep must be disabled at the systemd level ([logind.conf(5)](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/logind.conf.html); [Debian Wiki, Suspend](https://wiki.debian.org/Suspend)). The battery must be held well below full with firmware charge thresholds, around 40 to 60% for an always-plugged machine, where the laptop's maker supports them ([TLP battery FAQ](https://linrunner.de/tlp/faq/battery.html); [ASUS support](https://www.asus.com/support/faq/1032726/)). Each hub component must be its own unit with `Restart=on-failure`, started at boot without a login ([systemd.service(5)](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/systemd.service.html)). And disk encryption must be reconciled with unattended reboots, since a passphrase prompt stops the hub from returning by itself; TPM2-bound unlock restores unattended boots at the cost of weaker protection when the whole machine is stolen ([systemd-cryptenroll(1)](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/systemd-cryptenroll.html)). The hub holds the certificate authority key, broker credentials and Wi-Fi keys, so some encryption is warranted. Spare phones become wall panels with stock app pinning on Android or Guided Access on iOS ([Google Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9455138); [Apple Support 111795](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111795)). Devices without a built-in charge limit should charge through a hub-controlled plug, because users report swollen batteries in always-plugged tablets ([openHAB thread, bloated battery](https://community.openhab.org/t/bloated-battery-in-tablet-fire-hazard/145821)).

## EUR 100 buys one of everything the first slice needs

The 2026 market changed what "cheap and local" means. IKEA's cheapest hardware moved to Matter over Thread. Flashing open firmware onto cloud devices became the hard path: Tuya's over-the-air exploit has been patched since February 2022, the Shelly Plug S Gen3 can be reflashed only after destructive opening, and the Shelly-to-Tasmota converter was archived in 2025 ([tuya-cloudcutter](https://github.com/tuya-cloudcutter/tuya-cloudcutter); [ESPHome devices: Shelly Plug S Gen3](https://devices.esphome.io/devices/Shelly-Plug-S-Gen3); [tasmota/mgos32-to-tasmota32](https://github.com/tasmota/mgos32-to-tasmota32)). The rule is now to buy devices that are open by design: Zigbee devices with their own Zigbee2MQTT definition, stock firmware with a local interface, or products sold pre-flashed. For a rented flat every mains part is a bought, certified, plug-in product, and home-built electronics stay on the low-voltage side; anything behind a wall plate formally requires a registered installer in Germany, to take one country ([§ 13 NAV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/nav/__13.html)).

The first round should buy one of each thing needed to prove the whole chain: Zigbee2MQTT with a bulb group and a bound button, one metered plug, and ESPHome nodes that publish sensor state.

The price-verified lines (coordinator, bulbs, the plug at its offer price, two C3 boards, the PIR and the EUR 7.95 sensor slot) total **EUR 74.60**, or EUR 81.55 with one shipping charge, which leaves about EUR 18 for the button and any second shop's postage. The margin depends on promotions. At the plug's list price the same basket rises by EUR 14.34, and the levers are then the Shelly Duo Wi-Fi bulbs, which save about EUR 15 but lose binding and mesh routing and add a Shelly adapter to the hub, or deferring the button. The C3 SuperMini is the budget board and carries the clone Wi-Fi risk noted earlier; an ESP32-C6 DevKitC for the node that must be dependable adds EUR 6.

Three choices in the table are deliberate. Zigbee bulbs are worth their premium because Zigbee binding lets a remote control a bulb group directly, even when the hub, Zigbee2MQTT or the coordinator is down ([Zigbee2MQTT: Binding](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/guide/usage/binding.html)), and that hub-independent path is what keeps a home with a young, self-built hub usable by everyone in it. The wall-switch problem is handled without touching the wiring: the switch stays on, the bulb's power-on behaviour is set so a power cut does not light every lamp, and the stick-on remote sits beside it ([Zigbee2MQTT: Innr RB 279 T](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/devices/RB_279_T.html)). That is why the bindable Hue dimmer is the better button and the Sonoff SNZB-01P, which cannot bind, the cheaper one ([Zigbee2MQTT: Hue dimmer switch gen 2](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/devices/929002398602.html); [Zigbee2MQTT: SNZB-01P](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/devices/SNZB-01P.html)). And a Zigbee plug is preferred over the Shelly because it needs no extra adapter in the hub and routes the mesh, while generic TS011F plugs are to be avoided because newer firmware reports power only when polled ([Zigbee2MQTT: TS011F_plug_1](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/devices/TS011F_plug_1.html)). On a floor or table lamp the plug keeps the lamp's own switch and adds energy data, where a bulb would add dimming and colour temperature. Every exact model should be checked against Zigbee2MQTT's device list before ordering.

Later rounds are bought per need. For the **curtain**, two retrofit drives ride an existing track without drilling. The Aqara Curtain Driver E1 Track Version is Zigbee, appears in Zigbee2MQTT with position, battery and light level, moves up to 12 kg and fits only U- and I-shaped rails ([Aqara EU: Curtain Driver E1](https://www.aqara.com/eu/product/curtain-driver-e1/); [Zigbee2MQTT: ZNCLBL01LM](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/devices/ZNCLBL01LM.html)); its EU price and current availability could not be confirmed. The SwitchBot Curtain 3 costs EUR 89.99 and is controlled locally over Bluetooth from an ESP32 running OpenMQTTGateway, with no hub ([SwitchBot EU store](https://eu.switch-bot.com/products/switchbot-curtain-3); [OpenMQTTGateway: BLE](https://docs.openmqttgateway.com/use/ble.html)); whether its first calibration works without SwitchBot's app is not confirmed. The Aqara is the better fit for this stack if it can be bought. Either way the first step is mechanical: measure the rail profile, weigh the curtain and count the panels, since a curtain that opens from the middle needs two drives. RF track motors driven from the CC1101 stick are a second step, because they replace the rail and report no position.

For **LED strips**, WLED on an ESP32 controller fed by a closed, certified plug-in adapter keeps the only mains part bought and everything else low-voltage; WLED advises against ESP8266 for new installations, and pre-flashed Athom controllers cost US$7.84 to US$23.80 and ship from China ([WLED: compatible controllers](https://kno.wled.ge/basics/compatible-controllers/); [athom.tech WLED](https://www.athom.tech/wled)). The controller is chosen once the existing strips' voltage and type are known. Battery Zigbee sensors cost EUR 10.95 each at the same maker shop ([hobbyelectronica: Zigbee en Matter](https://www.hobbyelectronica.nl/product-categorie/platformen/zigbee-matter/)); Sonoff models are the lower-risk default on a mixed mesh, since Aqara end devices can drop off behind some routers ([Zigbee2MQTT: WSDCGQ12LM](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/devices/WSDCGQ12LM.html)). The router and the Ethernet coordinator follow when the network or the radio position demands them, and a voice satellite follows a spare Android phone, with the reSpeaker Lite kit at US$29.90 as the cheaper open alternative to the EUR 59 reference device ([Seeed reSpeaker Lite](https://www.seeedstudio.com/ReSpeaker-Lite-Voice-Assistant-Kit-p-5929.html)).

Some purchases are worth refusing outright: IKEA's current range, until a Thread radio is a deliberate decision; cloud-bound Wi-Fi devices bought in the hope of reflashing them; Bluetooth Tuya Fingerbots, which need a key from the vendor's cloud ([PlusPlus-ua/ha_tuya_ble](https://github.com/PlusPlus-ua/ha_tuya_ble)); in-wall modules, which a rental rules out; and a dedicated AI computer before the laptop has been benchmarked. All prices above come from manufacturer stores and one EU maker shop read on 25 November 2026, in Black-Friday week. Marketplaces and most EU resellers could not be checked, so every figure needs a second look before an order.

## Conclusion

The language model turns out to be the least load-bearing part of this "AI smart home". On the hardware that is always on, it is the slowest and least reliable component, and both the latency evidence and the safety evidence push work away from it and into a matcher, a registry and rules. What a custom hub buys is therefore not more intelligence but a model interface narrow enough for a 4B model to be trusted with, and the order of investment follows from that: self-description and the registry first, deterministic rules second, speech third, the model last. The same logic says where money would matter. The purchase that changes the experience is not a voice satellite or a mini PC but a host with fast memory, and that question stays closed until the laptop has been measured with a real, cached prompt.

The custom route's risk is bounded because its weakest assumptions are cheap to test before anything is bought. Four experiments settle them: the laptop's prompt-processing speed on CPU and integrated GPU, a bilingual command suite across the candidate models, the two M5 devices under ESPHome, and the private certificate with microphone access on each phone. Each has a named fallback, from a 2B-class model or Mac-only inference to plain development boards and a public-domain certificate, and the largest risk, the voice server, has two: the xiaozhi firmware or the hybrid with Home Assistant as device and app layer, which stays open because nothing at the edge is specific to the hub. One trend deserves a place in the design now. The cheapest mass-market devices have moved to Thread, and the EU's Cyber Resilience Act, whose main obligations apply from December 2027 ([European Commission: Cyber Resilience Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act)), gives vendors a further reason to sign and lock firmware. The registry should therefore treat Zigbee2MQTT and MQTT discovery as two sources among several and leave room for a Matter Server as the third.
