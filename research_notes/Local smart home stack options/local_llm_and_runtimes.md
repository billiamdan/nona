## 1. Which Gemma and Qwen releases exist as of November 2026, and what are their properties?

### Takeaway
Gemma 4 (2 April 2026, Apache 2.0; E2B, E4B, 26B A4B, 31B, plus a 12B "Unified" model added 3 June 2026) is the current Gemma generation and nothing newer was found by late November 2026; it is the only one of the two families with audio input in home-server sizes (E2B, E4B, 12B). Qwen's newest open small models are still the Qwen3.5 small series (0.8B/2B/4B/9B, about 2 March 2026, vision but no audio), while the 27B class advanced through Qwen3.6-27B (April 2026) to Qwen3.8-27B (August 2026); Qwen3.7 and Qwen3.5-Omni were not released as open weights.

### Cited Findings

#### Gemma family
- Gemma 4 launched on 2 April 2026 with four models (E2B, E4B, a 26B mixture-of-experts and a 31B dense model) and, for the first time for Gemma, an Apache 2.0 licence instead of Google's more restrictive Gemma terms (SS) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-gemma-4-open-models-apache-2-launch); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/02/googles_gemma_4_open_weights/)
- Parameter counts on the model card: E2B 2.3B effective / 5.1B including embeddings; E4B 4.5B effective / 8B; 12B Unified 11.95B; 26B A4B 25.2B total with 3.8B active (8 of 128 experts active plus 1 shared); 31B dense 30.7B — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- Context windows: 128K tokens for E2B and E4B; 256K for 12B, 26B A4B and 31B — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- Modalities: text and image on all sizes; audio natively on E2B, E4B and 12B only; video handled as frames. Audio input is limited to 30 seconds and video to 60 seconds at one frame per second. Output is text only — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- Languages: pre-training covers "over 140 languages", with out-of-the-box support for 35+ languages — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- Tool calling and reasoning: the card states native support for structured tool use; thinking is configurable and is switched on by placing a `<|think|>` token at the start of the system prompt — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- Licence Apache 2.0; training-data cutoff January 2025 — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- Google's approximate memory to load weights (including 20% overhead) at BF16 / 8-bit / 4-bit (Q4_0): E2B 11.4 / 5.7 / 2.9 GB; E4B 17.9 / 8.9 / 4.5 GB; 12B 26.7 / 13.4 / 6.7 GB; 26B A4B 57.7 / 28.8 / 14.4 GB; 31B 69.9 / 34.9 / 17.5 GB. All 26B parameters of the MoE must be loaded even though only about 4B are active — [Gemma 4 overview](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/)
- Vendor agent benchmark Tau2 (average of 3): 31B 76.9%, 26B A4B 68.2%, 12B 69.0%, E4B 42.2%, E2B 24.5%, versus Gemma 3 27B (no thinking) 16.2%. MMMU Pro: 31B 76.9%, 26B A4B 73.8%, 12B 69.1%, E4B 52.6%, E2B 44.2% — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- Gemma 4 12B was published on 3 June 2026. It is encoder-free: raw 16 kHz audio is cut into 40 ms frames and linearly projected into the LLM input space (no Conformer layers as in E2B/E4B), and a 35M-parameter embedder replaces the vision tower. It targets laptops with 16 GB of VRAM or unified memory, cites speech recognition and diarisation as audio uses, and shipped with day-one support in LM Studio, Ollama, llama.cpp, MLX, LiteRT-LM and vLLM — [Gemma 4 12B developer guide](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemma-4-12b-the-developer-guide/); [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemma-4-12b/)
- Google released Gemma 4 multi-token-prediction (MTP) drafter models on 16 April 2026, before the 12B model (SS) — [NYU Shanghai RITS summary](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/google-releases-gemma-4-12b-frontier-multimodal-ai-on-a-laptop/)
- The Hugging Face listing of Google's Gemma repositories shows official quantisation-aware-trained (QAT) Q4_0 GGUF repositories for E2B, E4B, 26B A4B and 31B (created 1 May 2026) and for 12B (5 June 2026), plus "-assistant" variants; the newest generative Gemma repositories in the listing are the gemma-4-12B ones (created 23 May to 5 June 2026). No Gemma 5 or other newer generative Gemma appears — [Hugging Face API listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=google&search=gemma&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=60)
- Ollama's `gemma4` library lists tags e2b (4.6–7.5 GB), e4b (6.6–9.5 GB, also `latest`), 12b (7.7–8.0 GB), 26b (16–19 GB) and 31b (19–20 GB), with separate `-mlx` tags and capability badges for vision, tools, thinking and audio; the page showed 29.8M downloads — [Ollama gemma4 library](https://ollama.com/library/gemma4)
- Gemma 3n (repositories created May–June 2025): E2B and E4B "effective" sizes, 32K-token context, images encoded to 256 tokens each, audio at 6.25 tokens per second, speech recognition and speech translation, training data covering over 140 languages; distributed under Google's Gemma terms of use rather than Apache — [Gemma 3n model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-3n/model-card/); [Hugging Face API listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=google&search=gemma&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=60)
- Gemma 3 270M repositories were created on 5 August 2025 — [Hugging Face API listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=google&search=gemma&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=60)
- FunctionGemma is a function-calling specialisation of Gemma 3 270M that is "intended to be fine-tuned for your specific function-calling task". Zero-shot BFCL scores on the card: Simple 61.6, Multiple 63.5, Parallel 39. On Google's Mobile Actions set the base model scored 58% and the fine-tune 85% (SS) — [FunctionGemma model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/functiongemma/model_card?hl=en); [Google DeepMind page](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/functiongemma/)
- FunctionGemma was announced on 18 December 2025; unlike Gemma 4 it is distributed under the Gemma licence (repository tagged `license:gemma`, access gated), not Apache 2.0 — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/developers/functiongemma/); [Hugging Face repository metadata](https://huggingface.co/google/functiongemma-270m-it)
- File sizes of Google's official QAT Q4_0 GGUF builds: Gemma 4 E2B 3.35 GB plus a 0.99 GB multimodal projector; E4B 5.15 GB plus 0.99 GB; 12B 6.98 GB plus 0.18 GB — [E2B files](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E2B-it-qat-q4_0-gguf); [E4B files](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E4B-it-qat-q4_0-gguf); [12B files](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B-it-qat-q4_0-gguf)
- File sizes of community Q4_K_M GGUF builds of Qwen3.5: 2B 1.28 GB, 4B 2.74 GB, 9B 5.68 GB, each with an optional vision projector of 0.67–0.92 GB — [Qwen3.5-2B GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-2B-GGUF); [Qwen3.5-4B GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-4B-GGUF); [Qwen3.5-9B GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-9B-GGUF)
- EmbeddingGemma 2, a 740M-parameter multimodal embedding model for phones and small boards, also exists (date not shown in the result) (SS) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/embeddinggemma-2-on-device-europe)

#### Qwen family
- Qwen3 was released on 29 April 2025 **[pre-mid-2025]** in 0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, 32B, 30B-A3B and 235B-A22B sizes under Apache 2.0, with support for 100+ languages and a thinking switch (`enable_thinking`, or `/think` and `/no_think` in the prompt). The Qwen3-2507 refresh (July–August 2025) added 256K context for the 4B, 30B-A3B and 235B models. Minimum runtime versions: llama.cpp b5401, Ollama v0.9.0, mlx-lm 0.24.0 — [Qwen3 GitHub](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3)
- Qwen3-VL (23 September to 21 October 2025): 2B, 4B, 8B, 32B, 30B-A3B and 235B-A22B, each in Instruct and Thinking editions, Apache 2.0, image and video input (no audio), native 256K context extendable to 1M, OCR in 32 languages — [Qwen3-VL GitHub](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL)
- Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (September 2025): Apache 2.0, text/image/audio/video in, text and speech out, 119 text languages, speech input in about 19 languages and speech output in 10; cookbooks cover sound analysis, music, mixed audio and "audio function call". Theoretical minimum GPU memory at BF16 is 78.85 GB for a 15-second video in the Instruct variant — [Qwen3-Omni model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct); release timing per [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/alibaba-expands-qwen3-family-with-1-trillion-parameter-max-open-weights-qwen3-vl-and-qwen3-omni-voice-model/) (SS)
- Qwen3.5 repositories were created between 16 February and 28 February 2026: 397B-A17B (16 Feb), 122B-A10B, 35B-A3B and 27B (24 Feb), 9B and 4B (26–27 Feb), 2B and 0.8B (28 Feb) — [Hugging Face API listing for Qwen](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=Qwen&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=60)
- The Qwen3.5 small series (0.8B, 2B, 4B, 9B) was announced on 2 March 2026 under Apache 2.0 (SS) — [NYU Shanghai RITS summary](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/qwen-3-5-small-models-9b-parameters-that-beat-120b/); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)

### Inferences
- For this project the live candidates are: Gemma 4 E2B/E4B and Qwen3.5-2B/4B for about 8 GB; Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma 4 12B for 16 GB; Gemma 4 26B A4B or 31B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8-27B for 24–48 GB. Older generations (Gemma 3, Gemma 3n, Qwen3, Qwen2.5) are superseded in the same memory footprint on the vendors' own agent benchmarks (for example Gemma 3 27B at 16.2% on Tau2 versus Gemma 4 E4B at 42.2%).
- If audio input to the main model matters, the choice within these two families is effectively Gemma 4 E2B, E4B or 12B; Qwen offers vision at every size but its only open audio-capable model (Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B) is too heavy for a 16–32 GB host at full precision.
- Qwen3.5 and later think by default, which is the opposite of Gemma 4 (thinking only when `<|think|>` is present). A home agent that needs sub-two-second responses should disable Qwen thinking explicitly at the runtime or template level.
- Ollama's default tags for Gemma 4 E2B/E4B (4.6–9.5 GB) are far larger than Google's 4-bit figures (2.9 and 4.5 GB), so the default tags are probably not the leanest quantisation; on an 8 GB host the official QAT Q4_0 GGUF is the safer choice.
- Qwen's cadence in 2026 moved the open frontier at 27B (3.5 → 3.6 → 3.8) but left the small series at 3.5; a small Qwen3.8 release would change the 8–16 GB recommendation.

### Gaps
- Gemma 3's own release date (March 2025) and licence text were not re-verified in this session; the Gemma 3 model-card URL returned 404.
- A "Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash" dated 18 September 2026 was mentioned once in a garbled aggregator summary; no such repository appears in the Hugging Face listing, so its existence and openness are unconfirmed.
- Multilingual quality: only capability claims were found (140+/35+ languages for Gemma 4, 201 for Qwen3.5). No independent head-to-head for specific non-English languages or for non-English tool calling was found; one secondary blog claims Gemma 4 31B leads Qwen3.5-27B on MMMLU 88.4% versus 85.9% but this was not verified.
- No published audio-task benchmark for Gemma 4's audio input was found, and the model card does not say whether non-speech sounds are understood.
- Whether Ollama exposes Gemma 4 audio input through its API is unclear: the library page carries an "audio" badge but its tag table lists only text and image inputs.

## 2. Which other open-weight models in the 1–32B range are strong at tool calling in late 2026?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced alternatives are Liquid AI's LFM3-1.7B (27 October 2026; vendor BFCL-V4 of 71.39 at about 1 GB), NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B (23 October 2026; a Gemma 4 E4B post-train with better "when to call" behaviour), IBM Granite 4.1 3B/8B (29 April 2026, Apache 2.0) and Mistral's Ministral 3 (December 2025, Apache 2.0), with GLM-4.7-Flash and gpt-oss-20b in the 12–18 GB class. Phi-4-mini and Gemma 3 4B trail in independent tests, and dedicated function-calling fine-tunes (xLAM-2, Hammer 2.1) failed in a standard OpenAI-compatible runtime because of template mismatch.

### Cited Findings
- Independent 40-case test of 13 models (19–20 March 2026; LM Studio 0.4.6, Q4_K_M, eight tools, deterministic scoring, temperature 0): Qwen3.5 4B 97.5%; GLM-4.7-Flash (18 GB) 95.0%; Nemotron Nano 4B (4.2 GB) 95.0%; Mistral Nemo 12B 92.5%; Qwen3 8B 85.0%; GPT-OSS 20B (12 GB) 85.0%; Nemotron 3 Nano MoE (25 GB) 85.0%; DeepSeek-R1-Distill 14B 57.5%; Phi-4 Mini 57.5%; Gemma 3 4B QAT 55.0%; Mistral Small 3.2 24B 42.5%; Hammer 2.1 7B 20.0%; xLAM-2 8B 15.0%. The test predates Gemma 4 — [J.D. Hodges](https://www.jdhodges.com/blog/local-llms-on-tool-calling-2026-pt1-local-lm/)
- The same author attributes the low scores of Hammer 2.1, xLAM-2 and Mistral Small 3.2 mainly to chat-template and output-format incompatibility with LM Studio's API rather than model quality, and notes DeepSeek-R1-Distill spent tokens reasoning about whether to call a tool — [J.D. Hodges](https://www.jdhodges.com/blog/local-llms-on-tool-calling-2026-pt1-local-lm/)
- LFM3 (27 October 2026) is a family of six roughly 2B open-weight models from one 1.7B base: LFM3-1.7B (text, 256K context), LFM3-VL-2B, LFM3-Audio-2B (speech in and out in 18 languages, with tool calling), LFM3-Omni-2B (2.2B; vision, audio and text in, text and speech out) and two "decision" models (d1-2B and d1-Omni-2B). The text model fits in about 1 GB at 4-bit and runs on llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM and SGLang — [Liquid AI blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)

### Inferences
- Across three independent vendor tables (Qwen, Liquid, NVIDIA) and one independent test, Qwen3.5-4B is consistently at or near the top among roughly 4B general models for tool execution, while Gemma 4 E4B is consistently lower on multi-turn agent benchmarks (Tau2 38–42%) but better than Qwen3.5-4B on deciding when not to call a tool (When2Call 62.9 versus 37.8). ## 3. How reliable is tool calling in small local models, and what improves it?

### Takeaway
In 2026 tests, 4–9B models choose the right tool from a handful of options almost every time (85–97% overall pass rates with about eight tools), but multi-step chains, argument precision and knowing when not to call remain weak, and failures are often caused by runtime templates and parsers rather than the model. Grammar-constrained decoding guarantees valid syntax but does not fix wrong choices and can reduce correctness for models of about 3B and below; the measurable gains come from fewer tools and entities in context, higher-precision quantisation, thinking switched off for latency, validation-and-retry scaffolding, and task-specific fine-tunes.

### Cited Findings

#### Benchmark evidence
- The official Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard describes V4 but was last updated on 26 August 2025 (SS), so it does not cover 2026 small models — [BFCL leaderboard](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)
- In the 13-model test (March 2026), multi-tool calls were the main differentiator among models above 85%: Qwen3 8B and GPT-OSS 20B passed only 2 of 8 multi-tool cases, and Qwen3.5 4B and GLM-4.7-Flash sometimes issued both calls in parallel when the second depended on the first — [J.D. Hodges](https://www.jdhodges.com/blog/local-llms-on-tool-calling-2026-pt1-local-lm/)
- ToolCall-15 on an 8 GB laptop GPU (16 April 2026, llama.cpp b8816, temperature 0): Qwen3.5-9B scored 26/30 (Tool Selection 6/6, Parameter Precision 4/6, Multi-Step Chains 4/6, Restraint 6/6, Error Recovery 6/6); a 27B model on a larger GPU scored 29/30. Two failures came from XML-like tool calls that llama.cpp did not parse — [Architecture & Performance blog](https://architecture-performance.fr/en/blog/running-a-local-llm-on-a-consumer-gpu-8-gb-vram)
- CPU-only test of Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B (27–28 October 2026; 20 prompts, 5 mock tools): with thinking off, correct tool or correct no-tool in 60/60 runs and exact arguments in 57/60 (the only consistent error was resolving the relative date "Friday"); with thinking on, 20/20 on both, at about five times the latency (13.1 s versus 2.1–3.6 s median) — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)
- Vendor Tau2 results show a steep size effect inside one family: Gemma 4 E2B 24.5%, E4B 42.2%, 12B 69.0%, 31B 76.9% — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/)
- A preprint (23 August 2026) reports that moving function calling out of the main LLM into a smaller dedicated model using an instruction-following format outperforms native and prompt-based function calling and stays robust under aggressive quantisation (abstract only; no figures extracted) — [arXiv 2608.22472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22472)

#### Community and home-automation evidence
- Home Assistant's own documentation warns that "smaller models are more likely to make mistakes", that smaller models may chat poorly when tools are enabled, and that only tool-capable models can use tools — [Home Assistant Ollama integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/ollama/)
- Home Assistant's September 2025 blog says local models using 8 GB or more of VRAM were "nearly keeping up" with cloud models on the community Home LLM Leaderboard, which includes an "assist-mini" set to "give small local LLMs a fighting chance"; Assist handles known commands deterministically first and sends only the rest to the model — [Home Assistant blog](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/09/11/ai-in-home-assistant)
- A long-running community thread (October 2025 to 2026) reports that Ollama's default low-quantisation tags caused basic tool-call failures and that higher-quantisation GGUF files from Hugging Face run under llama.cpp were "much improved"; on the author's private 165-case benchmark Nemotron Bolt E4B scored 107 (0.98 s average turn) and Gemma 4 26B-A4B QAT scored 118 (1.25 s) — [Home Assistant community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860)
- In the same thread one user found a non-thinking Qwen 14B unreliable with 53 exposed entities at 8K context ("context overrun"); doubling context helped but did not fix it, and Qwen3 4B Instruct still sometimes targeted the wrong device. The author exposes 32 entities and recommends grouping devices — [Home Assistant community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860)
- The same author states "the prompt will make or break your voice experience": per-service sections, an explicit example of the desired output, explicit "always call the tool" rules for some question types, and a decision hierarchy for unclear or background speech — [Home Assistant community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860)
- On an Intel N150 (April–July 2026) Granite 3.1 3B and a ternary 8B model invented values and ignored instructions, Qwen3 4B Instruct succeeded only with `/no_think`, and Gemma 4 E4B gave the best phrasing; structured output via Home Assistant's AI Task returned empty objects until each field had a `description` — [Home Assistant community thread (N150)](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/running-a-local-llm-on-an-intel-n150-mini-pc-for-home-assistant-a-practical-journey/1007724)
- Gemma 4 tool calling was broken at launch in Ollama v0.20.0 (parser failures, streaming dropping tool calls) and needed llama.cpp template and tokenizer fixes (PRs #21326 and #21343) built from source in early April 2026 (SS) — [GitHub gist](https://gist.github.com/daniel-farina/87dc1c394b94e45bb700d27e9ea03193); related diagnosis at [Hugging Face forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/gemma-4-bug-fixes-and-research-request/176979)
- One hands-on test found Gemma 4 E4B "didn't call any tool at all" for a file-writing request, and another found it lost to Qwen3.5 9B on multi-tool tasks even after Ollama tuning (single-author tests) (SS) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/vystartasv/google-said-it-had-native-function-calling-i-tested-it-54i7); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/onewallai/9-reasons-qwen359b-outshines-larger-models-for-local-agents-on-rtx-5070-ti-5c69)
- A Home Assistant guide rates Qwen 3.5 9B at Q4 as the "best balance of speed and reliability" and advises skipping think-mode models (SS) — [InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/home-assistant-local-llm-guide/)

#### Constrained decoding with JSON-schema grammars
- "The Constraint Tax" (20 May 2026; about 15,000 generations on Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and SmolLM2-1.7B): hard schema decoding raised schema validity from 61.5% to 100% but answer accuracy fell from 19.7% to 11.0%; on a calendar tool-call task Qwen2.5-1.5B dropped from 91.5% executable accuracy with prompt-only JSON to 48.0% under a hard tool-call schema, both at 100% validity. The author recommends "reason free, constrain late" and reporting validity and correctness separately — [arXiv 2605.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)
- A September 2026 benchmark of 0.6B–4B models across 14 structured-output tasks found constrained decoding eliminated structural failures (validity 78.6–92.9% to 100%) but semantic errors remained and multi-step function calling was not fixed (SS) — [arXiv 2609.23742](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23742)
- "Repair, Not Improvement" (August 2026) argues that restricting output to a grammar over declared tool names removes malformed output and gives small models a large gain in tool-call abstention settings (SS) — [arXiv 2608.13959](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13959)
- llama.cpp supports JSON-schema and GBNF grammar constraints in its server, and its function-calling doc warns that extreme KV-cache quantisation (for example `-ctk q4_0`) "can substantially degrade" tool calling; parallel tool calls are off by default — [llama-server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md); [llama.cpp function-calling doc](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/function-calling.md)

#### How many tools and entities
- On BFCL with Qwen2.5 7B, 3B and 1.5B, scaling candidate tools from fewer than 10 to 20 degraded all models, "especially ... the smaller models" (SS) — [arXiv 2603.11495](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11495)
- A practitioner test of six cloud models with up to 150 tools reported degradation starting between 25 and 50 tools (not peer reviewed) (SS) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/craigtracey/we-gave-llms-150-tools-heres-what-broke-2jaj)
- An adaptive tool shortlist beat always showing five tools (93.1% versus 87.1%) in a May 2026 study using a large cloud model (SS) — [arXiv 2605.24660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24660)
- Another study found larger models do better with all tools visible while smaller models are more sensitive to context length and benefit from shorter inputs and simpler tool selection (SS) — [arXiv 2511.11933](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11933)
- Home Assistant's Ollama integration defaults to an 8,192-token context (minimum 2,048), notes larger values are needed for homes with more exposed entities, and advises exposing only the entities the assistant needs — [Home Assistant Ollama integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/ollama/)
- A community guide (19 August 2026) stresses that a short spoken request becomes a much larger prompt once entity data and tool definitions are added, and that prompt processing can delay the first token by several seconds — [Home Assistant community guide](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/so-you-want-to-ai-in-home-assistant/1021777)

#### Mitigation techniques and fine-tunes
- Guardrail scaffolding (Forge, May 2026) on Ministral-3 8B: response validation plus rescue parsing +8–12 points, targeted retry nudges +6–9, step enforcement +5–8 on multi-step scenarios, context compaction +3–5 on long scenarios; letting the model freely choose between text and a tool call dropped completion sharply, which a synthetic "respond" tool avoids. The project's "53% to 99%" headline is not supported by the article's own best figure of about 86.5% — [DEV Community write-up of Forge](https://dev.to/monuminu/llm-agent-guardrails-the-engineering-playbook-for-taking-an-8b-local-model-from-53-to-99-on-18c)
- Renaming tool-schema components to names the model already knows from pretraining (PA-Tool, ACL 2026, training-free) improved accuracy by up to 17% on MetaTool and RoTBench and cut schema-misalignment errors (invented tool names) by 80% — [arXiv 2510.07248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07248)
- FunctionGemma 270M rose from 58% to 85% on Google's Mobile Actions set after fine-tuning (SS) — [FunctionGemma model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/functiongemma/model_card?hl=en)
- The home-llm project ships Home-Llama-3.2-3B and Home-FunctionGemma-270m fine-tunes, in-context-learning examples for general models (Qwen3, Llama 3, Mistral named), GBNF grammar support, auto-repair of malformed JSON tool calls and an agentic tool loop; v0.5.1 requires Home Assistant 2026.5.0 or newer. The page gives no accuracy figures — [home-llm GitHub](https://github.com/acon96/home-llm)
- Selora's fine-tunes on the community Home Assistant datasets (12 October 2026, temperature 0, self-reported): the 1.7B model scored 74.3% on "assist" (460 cases) model-only and 84.3% with its integration, and 93.9% / 100% on "assist-mini"; the 14B scored 80.4% / 88.0% on "assist". A control stub that never acts scored 77% on "assist", and single fused models scored well below adapter-swapped specialists (65.9% versus 80.4% model-only for the 14B) — [Selora blog](https://selorahomes.com/blog/selora-ai-llm-v0.6.1/)
- Selora's models "output one small JSON object" and never read or invent device state; the integration fills in live values, and the training corpus targets renamed devices and entity IDs taken from the home's own list — [Selora blog](https://selorahomes.com/blog/selora-ai-llm-v0.6.1/)

### Inferences
- A defensible reliability estimate for a current 4–9B model with about 5–10 well-described tools and a pruned entity list is "nearly always the right tool, usually the right arguments, unreliable on dependent multi-step chains", so multi-step work (for example composing an automation rule) is better decomposed in code or given to a larger model.
- Because a never-acting stub scores 77% on the Home Assistant "assist" set, headline accuracies in the 80s on that benchmark overstate real margin; "assist-mini" and model-only scores are more informative.
- Grammar constraints are best used late and narrowly: constrain the tool name and the enumerated entity or parameter values (which encode correctness, not just syntax), and let the model emit free text or reasoning before the constrained object rather than forcing the whole response into a schema from the first token.
- Enumerated entity IDs in the schema, short tool lists selected per request (retrieval or rule-based routing), and deterministic resolution of dates, units and percentages in code follow directly from the failure modes observed (wrong device, mis-resolved "Friday", parallelised dependent calls).
- Thinking improves argument accuracy but multiplies latency about fivefold on small hardware; a practical compromise is thinking off for device commands and thinking on only for rule-writing or ambiguous requests.
- Runtime and template maturity matter as much as the model: new model families typically need several weeks of runtime fixes before tool calling is dependable, so pin known-good runtime builds and re-run a private regression suite after every model or runtime update.

### Gaps
- The allenporter/home-assistant-datasets leaderboard itself (model-by-model scores) could not be retrieved; only third-party numbers computed on it were found.
- No controlled study was found that measures small-model accuracy as a function of the number of exposed smart-home entities; the 32-versus-53-entity observations are anecdotal.
- No study isolates the effect of JSON-schema constraints on 4–12B models from 2026 families; the quantified "constraint tax" evidence covers models of 1.7B and below.
- Few-shot example benefits are supported by practitioner reports and home-llm's design, not by a quantified 2026 study found in this session.

## 4. Local runtimes in late 2026: capabilities and suitability as an always-on service

### Takeaway
llama.cpp's `llama-server` is the most complete single runtime for an always-on agent backend (OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible endpoints, Jinja tool templates on by default, JSON-schema and GBNF constraints, image and audio input, parallel slots, prompt cache, multi-model router mode, idle sleep; MIT), and Ollama (MIT) is the easiest service to operate and has used an MLX engine on Apple Silicon since 2026. Raw MLX servers post the highest single-stream decode speeds on Apple Silicon in the cited tests, but `mlx_lm.server` is explicitly "not recommended for production"; LM Studio's headless `llmster` is free but proprietary; vLLM targets Linux GPU servers.

### Cited Findings

#### Ollama
- Licence MIT; REST API on `localhost:11434` — [Ollama GitHub](https://github.com/ollama/ollama)
- Latest release at the time of research: v0.40.18 (24 November 2026). Recent notes: Apple Silicon "now serves multiple requests at once" (v0.40.17, 10 November); recurrent and vision GGUF models can serve more than one request (v0.40.15); `/v1/responses` and Anthropic Messages endpoints are maintained; `ollama launch claude` supports deferred loading of MCP tools; speculative decoding with DFlash draft models on MLX (v0.40.12) — [Ollama releases](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/releases)
- OpenAI compatibility at `http://localhost:11434/v1/` covers chat completions, streaming, JSON mode, vision and tools (SS) — [Ollama OpenAI compatibility docs](https://docs.ollama.com/api/openai-compatibility)
- MLX engine: previewed in Ollama 0.19 on 30 March 2026 (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B decode 58 → 112 tokens/s and prefill 1,154 → 1,810 tokens/s against 0.18; requires a Mac with more than 32 GB at preview), with cache reuse across conversations, checkpoint snapshots and smarter eviction; later posts cover Ollama 0.30 GGUF support (5 June 2026), MLX performance updates (11 June) and multi-token prediction making Gemma 4 "up to 90% faster with coding agents" (29 June) — [Ollama blog: MLX](https://ollama.com/blog/mlx); [Ollama blog index](https://ollama.com/blog)
- A third-party article says v0.30.0 (13 May 2026) made MLX the default Apple Silicon path, which conflicts with Ollama's own 5 June post date for 0.30 (SS) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/jovan_chan_9500711396d4e6/ollama-v030-on-apple-silicon-what-the-stable-mlx-release-actually-changed-from-the-preview-25h7); contradicted on date by [Ollama blog index](https://ollama.com/blog)
- Keep-alive: models unload after 5 minutes by default; `keep_alive: -1` or `OLLAMA_KEEP_ALIVE` keeps them loaded. Concurrency: `OLLAMA_MAX_LOADED_MODELS` defaults to 3 × the number of GPUs (3 on CPU), `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL` defaults to 1 on most backends (auto on MLX), queue limit 512. - Service operation: environment set with `launchctl setenv` on macOS and `systemctl edit ollama.service` on Linux; binds to 127.0.0.1 by default; a "local only mode" (`OLLAMA_NO_CLOUD=1` or `"disable_ollama_cloud": true`) disables cloud models and web search — [Ollama FAQ](https://docs.ollama.com/faq)
- Structured outputs constrained to a JSON schema date from 6 December 2024 **[pre-mid-2025]**; a 2026 third-party changelog says XGrammar enforces them on the MLX runner and that "almost every release in 2026 carries a tool-calling fix" (SS) — [Ollama blog index](https://ollama.com/blog); [fazm.ai changelog summary](https://fazm.ai/t/ollama-release-notes-2026)
- Home Assistant's official local integration is Ollama; it defaults keep-alive to -1 and context to 8,192 tokens — [Home Assistant Ollama integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/ollama/)

#### llama.cpp (`llama-server`)
- Licence MIT — [llama.cpp GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp)
- Server features: OpenAI-compatible chat completions, responses and embeddings plus Anthropic Messages; tool calling via `--jinja` (enabled by default); `--json-schema`, the `json_schema` request field and `--grammar`; `--mmproj` multimodal projectors; `--parallel` slots with continuous batching on by default; `--cache-prompt` on by default, `--cache-reuse`, `--cache-ram` (default 8,192 MiB), `--slot-save-path`; `--sleep-idle-seconds`; router mode with `--models-dir`, `--models-preset`, `--models-max` (default 4), `--models-memory-margin` and on-demand autoload; `--reasoning on|off|auto` and `--reasoning-budget`; `--api-key`; Prometheus `--metrics`; `/health` — [llama-server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)
- Router mode hosts several models in one server, each in an isolated child process, routed by model name (SS) — [DeepWiki summary](https://deepwiki.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/6.3-router-mode-and-model-management)
- Tool calling: native handlers for many families (Llama 3.x, Hermes, Qwen 2.5, Mistral Nemo, Functionary, GPT-OSS and others) with a generic fallback that "may consume more tokens and be less efficient"; the page fetched does not mention Gemma 4 or Qwen3.5 by name — [llama.cpp function-calling doc](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/function-calling.md)
- Multimodal: image, audio and video input through `libmtmd`; listed models include Gemma 4 E2B/E4B (audio and vision), Gemma 4 26B-A4B/31B (vision), Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B and Qwen2.5-Omni (audio and vision), Qwen3-ASR 0.6B/1.7B, Voxtral Mini 3B and Ultravox 0.5 (audio). Gemma 4 12B, Qwen3-VL and Qwen3.5 were not in the list as fetched — [llama.cpp multimodal doc](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md)
- The server README still labels image and audio input as experimental — [llama-server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)
- Community practice: an N150 host ran two `llama-server` instances under systemd with staggered start-up, and a Home Assistant power user moved from Ollama to llama.cpp for performance — [Home Assistant community thread (N150)](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/running-a-local-llm-on-an-intel-n150-mini-pc-for-home-assistant-a-practical-journey/1007724); [Home Assistant community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860)

#### MLX (mlx-lm, mlx-vlm and third-party MLX servers)
- `mlx_lm.server` offers an OpenAI-style chat API with per-request model switching, LoRA adapters, draft-model speculative decoding and KV-cache quantisation, but is "not recommended for production as it only implements basic security checks"; the page does not document a tools parameter or `response_format`, and an experimental `server_v2` exists — [mlx-lm SERVER.md](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/SERVER.md)
- mlx-vlm (MIT) accepts text, images, video and audio; its OpenAI-compatible server exposes `/v1/chat/completions`, `/v1/responses`, `/v1/audio/transcriptions`, `/v1/realtime` and others, supports `json_schema` structured output and automatic prefix caching with in-memory and optional disk tiers, and lists Gemma 3n, Gemma 4, LFM3-Audio/Omni and Phi-4 Multimodal; general tool calling is not documented — [mlx-vlm GitHub](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm)
- Third-party MLX servers add what the reference server lacks: oMLX uses mlx-lm's tool parsers and a two-tier RAM+SSD KV cache (its author reports time to first token on repeated 50–100K prefixes falling from 30–90 s to 1–3 s) (SS); Rapid-MLX ships 18 tool-call parsers including Gemma 4's native format and Qwen 3/3.5 and caches repeated system prompts — [oMLX GitHub](https://github.com/jundot/omlx); [ModelFit](https://modelfit.io/blog/mlx-vs-ollama-mac-2026/); [DEV Community (Rapid-MLX)](https://dev.to/raullen_chai_76e18e9705b0/gemma-4-on-apple-silicon-85-toks-with-a-pip-install-299a)

### Inferences
- For an always-on Linux box (GPU, CPU or Vulkan iGPU), `llama-server` is the most capable choice because it combines grammar constraints, audio/vision input, prompt caching and a multi-model router in one MIT-licensed binary; Ollama is the lower-effort alternative and is what Home Assistant integrates natively.
- For the actual always-on host (a 16 GB Linux laptop without a dedicated GPU, per the scope update), `llama-server` is the better fit than Ollama: explicit control of the prompt cache and slots, separate thread counts for generation and prompt processing, GBNF grammars, optional Vulkan offload to the integrated GPU and a smaller memory overhead all matter more on CPU than on a GPU. Details and evidence are in section 10.
- On the M5 Pro MacBook, an MLX-backed path is likely fastest for generation. The practical options are Ollama (MLX engine, simplest service management), LM Studio's `llmster` with the MLX engine (parallel requests, GUI for testing) or a third-party MLX server with tool parsers; `llama-server` remains the better option when GBNF grammars, the multi-model router or Gemma 4 audio input are required.
- The May 2026 gap between raw mlx-lm and Ollama's MLX engine (163 versus 65 tokens/s) pre-dates Ollama's June MLX and MTP updates and its November parallel-request work, so it should be re-measured rather than assumed.
- Whatever runtime is chosen, the defaults that matter for a home agent are: pin the context length explicitly (Ollama's 4,096 default is too small once tools and entities are included), keep the model loaded (`keep_alive -1`), keep a stable system-prompt prefix so the KV cache is reused, bind to localhost and add an API key if exposed on the LAN.
- A launchd agent (macOS) or systemd unit (Linux) around `llama-server`, `ollama serve` or `llmster` is standard practice; no runtime-specific obstacle was found, but `mlx_lm.server`'s own warning argues against exposing it beyond localhost.

### Gaps
- No single source with November 2026 measurements compares Ollama (MLX), LM Studio (MLX), mlx-lm and llama.cpp on the same Apple chip, model and prompt; the available comparisons are from April–May 2026 and partly second-hand.
- LM Studio's support for grammar constraints, audio input, keep-alive/TTL behaviour and multi-model loading was not verified against its documentation in this session.
- Whether Gemma 4 12B's encoder-free audio path works in llama.cpp and Ollama today is unverified (the 12B is absent from llama.cpp's multimodal list as fetched, although Google lists llama.cpp as a day-one runtime).
- mlx-lm's licence and whether its newer `server_v2` adds tools and structured output were not checked.

## 5. Measured speed by hardware class (tokens per second and time to first token)

### Takeaway
At 4-bit, 4B-class models generate about 50–95 tokens/s on an M4 Pro-class Mac and about 45–60 on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395, a 9B model reaches about 60 tokens/s on an 8 GB NVIDIA laptop GPU, and the same 4B class manages roughly 10–15 tokens/s on CPU-only x86 or a Jetson Orin Nano Super and 4–5 tokens/s on an Intel N150. Dense 27–32B models run at about 10 tokens/s on Strix Halo while MoE models of similar total size are several times faster; no trustworthy direct measurements on an M5 Pro were found, only estimates and scaling from other chips.

- Scaling by memory bandwidth, an M5 Pro (307 GB/s) should generate at roughly half the rate of a 40-core M5 Max (614 GB/s) and about 12% faster than an M4 Pro (273 GB/s). Applying that to the M4 Pro measurements gives roughly 60–105 tokens/s for Gemma 4 E4B/E2B on the M5 Pro, with much faster prompt processing than the M4 Pro.
- For interactive voice control the binding constraint is time to first useful action, which is dominated by prompt processing of the system prompt, tool schemas and entity list. On GPU or Apple Silicon with a warm prompt cache this is around or under one second; on an N150 a few hundred tokens of uncached prompt cost about ten seconds, which rules that class out for live conversation but not for asynchronous tasks.
- The Linux laptop has no discrete GPU (scope update), so a 4B-class model at roughly 5–17 tokens/s with short replies is its realistic ceiling for interactive use, and 8–9B dense models (about 3–6 tokens/s) are suitable only for background tasks; section 10 gives the evidence and the techniques that make this usable for voice.
- A Jetson Orin Nano Super is slower than the 8 GB laptop GPU and limited to the 4B class by its 8 GB of shared memory; it suits a dedicated low-power small-model node rather than the main agent.

### Gaps
- No measured tokens-per-second or time-to-first-token figures on an M5 Pro (the available host) were found for Gemma 4 or Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8; published numbers are estimates that disagree by a factor of two. Measuring on the actual machine with `mlx_lm` and `llama-bench` is the only way to close this.
- A reportedly well-controlled MLX run of all Gemma 4 sizes on an M5 Max (short and 256K contexts) appeared in a search summary but could not be traced to a source because the session's search budget was exhausted; the same applies to Ollama's 29 June 2026 Gemma 4 MTP post.
- No measurement was found for a base Mac mini M4/M6 with 16–24 GB running current 8–12B models (only Llama 3.2 3B on an M4 with 32 GB), nor for Gemma 4 12B on any hardware.
- No measured N100 result and no measured 27–32B result on N100/N150, Jetson or 8 GB GPUs were found; those combinations are impractical on memory grounds.
- Time to first token is reported inconsistently across sources (different prompt lengths, cold versus warm cache), so the figures above are not directly comparable.

Tariffs and the GPU idle figure are assumptions, not sourced.
- Because the MacBook Pro M5 Pro with 48 GB is already owned, a dedicated box is only justified by availability (a laptop that travels or sleeps), not by capability; nothing under about $2,300 matches it for model size and speed.

## 7. Multimodal perception at home: audio and image input versus small dedicated classifiers

### Takeaway
Local models that accept audio now exist at practical sizes (Gemma 4 E2B/E4B/12B, LFM3-Audio/Omni, Voxtral Mini, Ultravox, Qwen2.5-Omni, and the much heavier Qwen3-Omni), but the 2026 evidence favours dedicated audio models for recognising household sounds: audio-text embedding and tagging models match or beat audio LLMs at sound-category identification, audio LLMs state wrong answers confidently, and LLM-only smart-home perception over-refuses and misses safety events. For camera snapshots, 4–9B vision-language models are practical on a GPU or Apple Silicon when a conventional detector triggers them, and are too slow on CPU-class hardware.

### Cited Findings
- Audio-capable open models with local runtime support: Gemma 4 E2B and E4B (audio and vision), Qwen2.5-Omni 3B/7B and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (audio and vision), Voxtral Mini 3B, Ultravox 0.5 and Qwen3-ASR 0.6B/1.7B (audio) are listed in llama.cpp's multimodal documentation; mlx-vlm lists Gemma 3n, LFM3-Audio, LFM3-Omni and Phi-4 Multimodal — [llama.cpp multimodal doc](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md); [mlx-vlm GitHub](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm)
- Gemma 4 accepts at most 30 seconds of audio per input; Google's 12B guide cites speech recognition and diarisation as audio uses, and Gemma 3n's card describes speech recognition, speech translation and the ability to "analyze sound-based data" without detailing non-speech tasks — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/); [Gemma 4 12B developer guide](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemma-4-12b-the-developer-guide/); [Gemma 3n model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-3n/model-card/)
- Qwen3-Omni's cookbooks cover sound analysis, music and mixed audio, but llama.cpp implements only its audio-input side and the full-precision model needs about 79 GB or more of GPU memory (SS for the llama.cpp detail) — [Qwen3-Omni model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct); [awesomeagents.ai](https://awesomeagents.ai/news/llama-cpp-three-audio-models-48-hours/)
- LFM3-Audio-2B takes speech in and out with tool calling; on Liquid's internal audio tool-calling test it scored 58.5 against 72.4 for Gemma 4 E4B (vendor) — [Liquid AI blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)
- Tiered benchmark of sound-source identification (3 August 2026; 2,242 clips, 23 fine-grained classes in 11 categories): the best closed-set LLM (Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview) reached 85.6% category F1 and 56.7% fine-grained F1; the open-weight Kimi-Audio-7B reached 67.5% and 32.9% and failed to answer 1.6% of samples; the taggers and embedding models SSLAM and CLAP "match or exceed" the best LLM at category level without being shown the candidate list; wrong LLM answers were stated confidently 92–100% of the time — [arXiv 2608.02397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02397)
- The same paper's table reports category accuracy of 89.1% for CLAP, 88.4% for SSLAM, 84.3% for PANNs, 77.8% for Whisper-AT and 52.1% for YAMNet (SS) — [arXiv 2608.02397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02397)
- With five overlapping sound sources YAMNet's accuracy fell to 63.8% against 81.5% for a baseline Audio Spectrogram Transformer and 93.2% for a fine-tuned one; with a single source all three exceeded 96% (SS) — [arXiv 2603.00395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00395)
- A UbiComp 2026 poster on smart-home agents (6 August 2026) compared traditional detectors, single multimodal-LLM agents and multi-agent mediation on ambient inputs (TV speech, on-screen text, overheard conversation, health-monitor triggers): detectors act on everything, while every multimodal-LLM configuration over-refuses, completing almost no genuine commands and missing a true fall in every case; an oracle that picks the right layer per case reaches 94.1% against 76.5% for the best single layer, and the authors argue for "learned routing and sensor fusion" rather than replacing detectors with a multimodal LLM — [arXiv 2608.05495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05495)
- For spoken commands, community practice uses dedicated speech-to-text ahead of the LLM: Qwen3-ASR 1.7B via llama.cpp (recommended by one power user), Gemma 4 E4B as a transcriber, or Parakeet V3 through ONNX; a CPU-only stack with Whisper small.en and Piper adds about 0.66 GB and yields a 4.1–4.6 s voice turn — [Home Assistant community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)
- One tester found Gemma 4 E2B's audio handling "garbled" on an M4 Pro (April 2026) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/akartit/i-tested-every-gemma-4-model-locally-on-my-macbook-what-actually-works-3g2o)
- Images: Frigate's documentation recommends Qwen3-VL (examples `qwen3-vl:8b-instruct` and `qwen3-vl:4b`) for event descriptions, notes that Qwen3.6/3.8 lack Qwen3-VL's DeepStack component and are worse at small details such as objects in hands, and says Ollama on CPU is "not recommended" because of inference times (SS) — [Frigate GenAI docs](https://docs.frigate.video/configuration/genai/genai_config)
- Vision quality indicators: MMMU Pro of 52.6% (Gemma 4 E4B), 69.1% (12B) and 76.9% (31B); MMMU-Pro of 65.4% (Qwen3.5 4B) and 69.2% (9B) from a different evaluator — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models) (SS)
- On an Intel N150, Qwen3-VL 4B generates at 4.7 tokens/s (about 18 s per request) — [Home Assistant community thread (N150)](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/running-a-local-llm-on-an-intel-n150-mini-pc-for-home-assistant-a-practical-journey/1007724)
- "Decision" models are emerging as a middle ground: Liquid's d1 models answer typed questions (yes/no, choices, scores, bounding boxes) about an image or audio clip in one pass with a calibrated probability, and d1-Omni-2B identifies 43 spoken languages; Ollama added decision-model support on 29 September 2026 — [Liquid AI blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3); [Ollama blog index](https://ollama.com/blog)

### Inferences
- For household sound events (glass break, alarm, doorbell, water running, appliance beeps) a small always-on classifier or audio-embedding model (CLAP, SSLAM, PANNs or a fine-tuned AST) is the better first stage: it is cheaper, runs continuously on CPU and is at least as accurate at category level. An audio LLM is best reserved for on-demand description of a clip that a detector has already flagged.
- YAMNet, the usual default, is the weakest of the classifiers in the 2026 comparisons and degrades with overlapping sources, so it should not be assumed adequate without testing on the home's own sounds.
- Gemma 4's audio encoder appears speech-oriented (speech recognition, translation, diarisation are the documented uses); its usefulness for non-speech household sounds is undocumented and should be treated as unproven.
- A 30-second audio limit and the cost of running an LLM per clip make continuous audio monitoring with an LLM impractical; event-triggered clips are the workable pattern.
- For cameras the same two-stage design applies: a conventional detector (motion or object detection) triggers a snapshot, and a 4–9B vision-language model describes it. This is comfortable on the M5 Pro or a GPU and too slow on an N150 or CPU-only host for anything interactive.
- Confidence statements from audio LLMs are not trustworthy (wrong answers were confident 92–100% of the time), so any automation triggered by a perception model should rely on calibrated classifier scores or corroborating sensors rather than the model's self-reported certainty.

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found that evaluates Gemma 4, LFM3-Audio/Omni or Qwen3-Omni specifically on household or environmental sound recognition; the 2026 comparison used Gemini models and Kimi-Audio-7B as the LLMs.
- No measured throughput or latency for local audio LLM inference on the candidate hosts was found.
- No head-to-head evaluation of small vision-language models against a dedicated detector for home-camera tasks was found; Frigate's guidance is qualitative.
- The licence and local-runtime maturity of the LFM3 audio and decision models are unverified.

## 8. Agent orchestration for small local models, lightweight frameworks and MCP

### Takeaway
For models under about 10B the best-supported pattern is a single agent with a small, curated tool set and deterministic scaffolding; in the largest 2026 study, routing-based multi-agent setups used about 1.8 times the tokens of a single agent and completed fewer tasks (72% versus 80%). Routing still helps when it is done by something cheap and reliable (rules, a classifier or a small fine-tuned router) that selects a specialised prompt, adapter or model per task, and MCP (stateless since the 2026-07-28 specification, with stdio still a standard transport) is a reasonable local tool interface with FastMCP as the mainstream Python implementation.

### Cited Findings

#### One agent versus router plus specialists
- "Rethinking Scale" (21 April 2026; 27 open models under 10B across 20 datasets, financial-domain tasks) compared a base model, a single-agent system with tools and a multi-agent system: completion rates 99.67%, 79.92% and 72.01%; tokens per sample 2,356, 8,040 and 14,618; average latency 12.39 s, 23.27 s and 22.76 s. Single-agent systems gave the best performance-cost balance, multi-agent gains were small, and failures were dominated by context-length overruns (single agent) and delegation failures (multi-agent); the authors recommend fallbacks to the base model when an agent stalls — [arXiv 2604.19299](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19299v1); also listed in the [ACL 2026 industry track](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.123.pdf) (SS)
- A contrasting study (January 2026, revised April 2026) found small multi-agent systems with one orchestrator and restricted sub-agent communication can outperform much larger single models on tool-intensive tasks; performance depends mainly on orchestrator capacity, reasoning helps most at the orchestrator, and enabling reasoning in sub-agents gives limited or negative benefit — [arXiv 2601.11327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11327)
- A small language model fine-tuned as an agent router (15 July 2026) reached a mean NDCG@10 of 0.771, ahead of two commercial small models used as intent-only routers (by 0.177 and 0.219), with a mean selection latency of 120.1 ms — [arXiv 2608.00030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00030)
- In the smart-home perception study, multi-agent mediation (voting, role specialists, cross-model arbitration) did not remove over-refusal, while an oracle router over layers reached 94.1% against 76.5% for the best single layer — [arXiv 2608.05495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05495)
- Practical examples of routing at small sizes: Home Assistant handles recognised commands deterministically and sends only the remainder to the model; an N150 owner keeps two models resident (about 10 GB of 16 GB) and routes factual tasks to one and spoken announcements to the other; Selora uses five LoRA specialists (command, automation, answer, clarification, utilities) on a shared base, which outperform a single fused model (80.4% versus 65.9% model-only on "assist" for the 14B) — [Home Assistant blog](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/09/11/ai-in-home-assistant); [Home Assistant community thread (N150)](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/running-a-local-llm-on-an-intel-n150-mini-pc-for-home-assistant-a-practical-journey/1007724); [Selora blog](https://selorahomes.com/blog/selora-ai-llm-v0.6.1/)
- Separating function calling from the conversational model (a dedicated small model that receives the task as an instruction) outperformed native function calling in an August 2026 preprint — [arXiv 2608.22472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22472)
- Deterministic scaffolding around a single small agent (validation, rescue parsing, retry nudges, step enforcement, context compaction) raised an 8B model from about 53% to 82–86.5% on an agent harness — [DEV Community write-up of Forge](https://dev.to/monuminu/llm-agent-guardrails-the-engineering-playbook-for-taking-an-8b-local-model-from-53-to-99-on-18c)

#### Lightweight frameworks that run offline
- Pydantic AI has a dedicated Ollama model class, and one guide reports schema-valid native output through Ollama's `json_schema` support (SS) — [Pydantic AI docs](https://ai.pydantic.dev/api/models/ollama); [guide](https://localaimaster.com/blog/pydantic-ai-ollama-agents)
- The OpenAI Agents SDK can be pointed at a local Chat Completions endpoint (a custom client with a local base URL, with tracing disabled) or routed through LiteLLM (SS) — [OpenAI cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/articles/gpt-oss/run-locally-ollama); [OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/agents-sdk-use-a-diff-model/1145349)
- Google's Agent Development Kit documents an Ollama path via LiteLLM and advises choosing a model with tool support; LiteLLM falls back to JSON-mode tool calls when native tool calling is unavailable, and open issues report tool calls not surfaced for some Ollama models and a Gemma 4 tool-result role that LM Studio rejects (SS) — [ADK Ollama guide](https://adk.dev/agents/models/ollama/); [LiteLLM Ollama docs](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/providers/ollama); [ADK issue](https://github.com/google/adk-python/issues/6482)
- LangGraph works with any OpenAI-compatible backend, so pointing it at a local server is a small configuration change (SS) — [KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/7-python-frameworks-for-orchestrating-local-ai-agents)
- One reviewer finds smolagents' dependence on model quality "significantly higher" than Instructor or Pydantic AI (single-author opinion) (SS) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/jangwook_kim_e31e7291ad98/python-ai-agent-library-comparison-2026-pydantic-ai-vs-instructor-vs-smolagents-practical-guide-43mg)
- Qwen's model cards recommend Qwen-Agent, which wraps tool use and supports MCP — [Qwen3 GitHub](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3); [Qwen3.5-9B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)
- An MLX server author lists PydanticAI, LangChain and smolagents as tested clients against local Gemma 4 and Qwen models — [DEV Community (Rapid-MLX)](https://dev.to/raullen_chai_76e18e9705b0/gemma-4-on-apple-silicon-85-toks-with-a-pip-install-299a)

#### Model Context Protocol in 2026
- The 2026-07-28 MCP specification (released 28 July 2026) makes the protocol stateless: the `initialize` handshake and `Mcp-Session-Id` header are retired and each request carries its own protocol version and capabilities; server-initiated requests are replaced by Multi Round-Trip Requests; Tasks moved into an extension; Roots, Sampling, Logging and the legacy HTTP+SSE transport are deprecated with at least twelve months of support; TypeScript, Python, Go and C# SDKs supported it at release — [MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)
- stdio remains one of two standard transports: the client launches the server as a subprocess and exchanges newline-delimited JSON-RPC messages over stdin/stdout, with stderr free for logs; the same framing may be reused over Unix domain sockets or TCP; because the protocol is stateless, a client can simply restart a crashed server and retry; clients should probe with `server/discover` to detect legacy servers — [MCP transports](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/transports); [MCP stdio transport](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/transports/stdio)
- FastMCP (Apache 2.0) turns typed Python functions into tools with automatically generated schemas and uses stdio by default when `run()` is called without arguments; its changelog shows v3.4.4 on 8 July 2026 and a v4 line reaching v4.2.5 on 23 November 2026 — [FastMCP GitHub](https://github.com/PrefectHQ/fastmcp); [FastMCP changelog](https://gofastmcp.com/changelog.md); [FastMCP v3 docs](https://gofastmcp.com/docs/v3/updates.md) (SS)
- Local hosts and clients: Home Assistant acts as both an MCP client (tools for its Assist agent) and an MCP server; LM Studio's stateful `/v1/chat` endpoint can use local MCP servers; Ollama's launcher supports deferred loading of MCP tools — [Home Assistant blog](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/09/11/ai-in-home-assistant); [LM Studio 0.4.0](https://lmstudio.ai/blog/0.4.0) (SS); [Ollama releases](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/releases)
- Vendor MCP-oriented scores for small models remain low: MCP-Atlas 32.57 for LFM3-1.7B and 41.37 for Qwen3.5-4B — [Liquid AI blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)

### Inferences
- The two academic findings are compatible: adding LLM-to-LLM delegation between small models costs tokens and reliability, whereas a capable orchestrator directing narrow workers helps. On a 48 GB host this suggests one mid-size model (27B class or a fast MoE) as the only component that plans, with small models used as stateless workers (speech-to-text, image description, quick intent classification), not as peers that negotiate.
- A "swarm" of small chatty agents is the least supported option for a home hub. A deterministic or classifier-based router in front of a few narrow prompts (device command, rule authoring, state question, perception) captures most of the benefit of specialisation while keeping each prompt and tool list short, which is exactly what small models need.
- The framework matters less than the scaffolding: no head-to-head framework comparison on small local models was found, and the measurable gains come from validation, retries, step limits and context control. A hand-written loop of a few hundred lines against an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, or Pydantic AI for typed outputs, is sufficient and avoids LiteLLM-layer incompatibilities reported with Ollama and LM Studio.
- MCP is a sensible internal contract for a home hub because it standardises tool schemas and discovery and works over stdio or a local socket with no network exposure. Its low benchmark scores for small models argue for presenting the model with only a per-request subset of MCP tools rather than the full catalogue.
- The stateless 2026-07-28 revision simplifies a local deployment (no session to keep alive, crash-restart is safe) but mixed-version clients and servers will coexist during the deprecation window, so the SDK or FastMCP version should be pinned and its spec level checked.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of Pydantic AI, smolagents, LangGraph, the OpenAI Agents SDK and Google ADK on the same small local model was found; an article advertising such benchmarks returned 404.
- The "Rethinking Scale" study used financial text tasks, not device control; no equivalent study for smart-home tool use was found.
- Whether FastMCP 4.x fully implements the 2026-07-28 specification, and what changed between FastMCP 3 and 4, could not be confirmed from the fetched pages.
- No measurements were found of how small local models perform when tools are supplied through MCP versus native function schemas.

## 9. Safety patterns for LLM-controlled physical devices

### Takeaway
Demonstrated attacks show that untrusted text reaching a tool-enabled model can operate real devices (a poisoned calendar invite made Gemini open windows, switch lights and activate a boiler in 2025), and published defences are architectural rather than model-based: restrict the model to choosing among pre-defined actions, keep untrusted content out of the privileged context, require confirmation for sensitive actions, expose only the entities needed, and keep safety-critical automations deterministic and independent of the model. No research specifically on injection through device names or sensor text was found, so that part rests on applying these general patterns.

### Cited Findings
- "Invitation Is All You Need" (August 2025): researchers used a poisoned Google Calendar invite so that, when the user later asked for a calendar summary and replied with an ordinary word such as "thanks", Gemini's home agent opened windows, turned lights off or activated a boiler; the authors say any assistant with tool access is susceptible — [arXiv 2508.12175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12175); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/cybersecurity/researchers-hacked-google-gemini-to-take-control-of-a-smart-home-201926464.html) (SS)
- Coverage attributes the root cause to models being unable to distinguish user instructions from reference content; Google's response included enhanced user confirmations for sensitive actions, URL sanitisation and prompt-injection classifiers (SS) — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/08/infosec_hounds_spot_prompt_injection/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/cybersecurity/researchers-hacked-google-gemini-to-take-control-of-a-smart-home-201926464.html)
- "Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections" (June 2025) proposes six patterns that isolate untrusted data from control flow, including action-selector (the model only maps a request onto predefined tool calls and never reads untrusted content), plan-then-execute (the allowed tool calls are fixed before untrusted data is read, although arguments can still be tampered with), LLM map-reduce and dual LLM (a privileged model plans and calls tools while a quarantined model with no tools processes untrusted data); the authors state the patterns trade utility for security (SS) — [arXiv 2506.08837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08837); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/13/prompt-injection-design-patterns/)
- A follow-up guide describes checking plans against security policies before execution (SS) — [arXiv 2509.08646](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08646)
- Ambient injection in the home: a 2026 pilot benchmark covers TV speech, on-screen text and overheard conversation that "merely looks like a command"; because labels skew toward inaction, an always-block policy scores 82% accuracy, so the authors report unsafe-execution and safe-completion rates separately — [arXiv 2608.05495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05495)
- Home Assistant limits model-driven control to entities explicitly exposed to Assist; the broader tool set that reaches all entities works only for administrators and is not available to voice satellites — [Home Assistant Ollama integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/ollama/)
- A community guide (August 2026) advises keeping deterministic automations independent of inference so lights and safety devices still work if a model is down, and warns that "packaging is not permissioning" — [Home Assistant community guide](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/so-you-want-to-ai-in-home-assistant/1021777)
- A production-oriented fine-tune constrains the model to emit one small JSON object, never lets it read or invent device state, and has the integration fill in live values; entity IDs come from the home's own list — [Selora blog](https://selorahomes.com/blog/selora-ai-llm-v0.6.1/)
- Observed failure modes relevant to safety: a model hallucinated weather values when a local intent ran with no weather entity exposed; models tend to end replies with questions, causing false-activation loops, which a decision hierarchy for unclear or background speech reduced; a small model consistently mis-resolved a relative date, which the tester recommends resolving in code — [Home Assistant community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/my-journey-to-a-reliable-and-enjoyable-locally-hosted-voice-assistant/944860); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)
- Models are poorly calibrated about their own errors: audio LLMs stated wrong answers confidently 92–100% of the time — [arXiv 2608.02397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02397)
- Runtime exposure: Ollama binds to 127.0.0.1 by default; `llama-server` supports API keys; `mlx_lm.server` implements only "basic security checks"; FastMCP offers strict Host and Origin validation as an opt-in — [Ollama FAQ](https://docs.ollama.com/faq); [llama-server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md); [mlx-lm SERVER.md](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/SERVER.md); [FastMCP v3 docs](https://gofastmcp.com/docs/v3/updates.md) (SS)
- Grammar constraints over declared tool names remove malformed or invented tool calls, and renaming schemas reduced invented tool names by 80%, which narrows what a manipulated model can emit (SS for the first) — [arXiv 2608.13959](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13959); [arXiv 2510.07248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07248)

### Inferences
- The action-selector pattern fits a home hub naturally: the model may only select a tool and arguments drawn from enumerated, pre-registered values (entity IDs, bounded numeric ranges), enforced by a JSON-schema grammar and re-validated in code. An injected instruction cannot then create a new capability; the worst case is a wrong but permitted action.
- Device names, room names, sensor text fields, media titles, calendar entries and transcribed background speech are all untrusted input. They should be passed as data (quoted values or IDs), stripped of instruction-like content and length-limited, and free text from devices should not be placed in the same context that can issue actions; where free text must be interpreted, a tool-less "quarantined" call should summarise it into a fixed schema first.
- Risk tiers follow from the Gemini case and Google's response: reversible low-risk actions (lights, sockets with benign loads) can execute directly; actions with physical or security consequences (heaters, locks, garage or curtain motors with pinch risk, anything unattended at night) should require explicit confirmation on a trusted channel, or be outside the model's allow-list entirely.
- Model-written automation rules are a delayed-action channel and deserve the same control: validate the JSON against a schema, check referenced entities and limits in code, and require user approval before a rule is activated.
- An append-only audit log of each request, the prompt hash, the tool call emitted, the validation result and the executed action makes mis-fires diagnosable and injections detectable; rate limits and a global "LLM control off" switch bound the damage of a loop or attack.
- Because always-refuse scores well on naive accuracy and small models over-refuse, a private evaluation should track unsafe executions and missed legitimate commands as separate numbers.

### Gaps
- No study or incident report was found that specifically tests prompt injection through device names, entity attributes or sensor text in a local smart-home agent.
- No source was found that documents audit-logging or confirmation-flow designs for local LLM home control beyond the vendor statements above; the specifics in the Inferences are general security engineering applied to this setting.
- The sixth pattern of the design-patterns paper and its "code-then-execute" description were not confirmed from the fetched material.
- No evidence was found on how susceptible Gemma 4 or Qwen3.5-class small models are to injection relative to large models.

## 10. CPU-only sizing for the always-on 16 GB Linux laptop, voice-latency techniques and tiered fallback to the Mac (scope update)

### Takeaway
On laptop-class CPUs, 1–2B models generate at roughly 18–35 tokens/s and 4B-class models at roughly 5–17 tokens/s, while a 4B-class model processes prompts at only about 50–200 tokens/s, so an uncached 1.5–2k-token tool-and-entity prompt costs on the order of 8–12 s on a recent CPU (and far longer on an old one) but about 1 s once the static prefix sits in the KV cache. A usable voice setup therefore combines a cached static prefix, a short tool list, thinking switched off, short or templated replies and a fast path (deterministic matching or a tiny function-calling model) in front of a 2–4B model, with the larger model on the Mac used opportunistically behind automatic failover.

#### Prompt and KV-cache reuse of a static prefix
- In `llama-server`, prompt caching is on by default: with `cache_prompt` true "the prompt is compared to the previous completion and only the 'unseen' suffix is evaluated", and responses report `tokens_cached`. A request can be pinned to a slot with `id_slot`; a slot's cache can be written to and restored from disk with `POST /slots/{id_slot}?action=save` and `?action=restore` when `--slot-save-path` is set; `--cache-reuse` enables reuse of shifted chunks; `--threads` and `--threads-batch` set thread counts for generation and for prompt processing separately — [llama-server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)
- Measured effect on CPU: with a system prompt plus five tool schemas of about 500 tokens, the very first request took 6.4 s before any output; with the prefix cached, the median time until a tool call was ready was 0.87–1.15 s and the median end-to-end tool call 2.1–3.6 s (about 20 generated tokens) — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/running-nvidia-nemotron-35-bolt-e4b-without-a-gpu-benchmark-results)

- Very small models cannot run agent loops: in a 27-model study Gemma-3-270m did much better as a plain model than in single- or multi-agent setups, which failed to run on most tasks — [arXiv 2604.19299](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19299v1)

#### Tiered setup: bigger model on the Mac when reachable, small model on the laptop otherwise
- LiteLLM documents automatic failover: "If a call fails after num_retries, LiteLLM falls back to another model group", and it also offers health-check-based routing — [LiteLLM docs](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/reliability)
- A Home Assistant custom component chains two conversation agents, "falling back if your primary agent fails to handle the intent" — [Fallback Conversation Agent](https://github.com/m50/ha-fallback-conversation)

- The quality gap that justifies escalating to the Mac: vendor Tau2 of 42.2% for Gemma 4 E4B against 69.0–76.9% for the 12B–31B models, and 26/30 on ToolCall-15 for a 9B model against 29/30 for a 27B model — [Gemma 4 model card](https://gemma.ai/models/gemma-4/model-card/); [Architecture & Performance blog](https://architecture-performance.fr/en/blog/running-a-local-llm-on-a-consumer-gpu-8-gb-vram)
- The largest small-model agent study recommends fallbacks to a simpler configuration when an agent stalls or runs out of context — [arXiv 2604.19299](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19299v1)

### Inferences
- Expected speeds on the unknown laptop CPU, by analogy: a recent laptop CPU (Lunar Lake class, AVX2/AVX-512, dual-channel DDR5) should give about 10–17 tokens/s for a 4B-class model and 25–35 for a 2B-class model; a five-to-ten-year-old dual-channel DDR4 laptop about 5–7 and 7–10. Dense 8–9B models land at 3–6.5 tokens/s even on server CPUs, which rules them out for live voice on this host.
- Time to first token for a 1.5–2k-token tool and entity prompt, derived from the prefill rates above (my arithmetic): cold on a recent CPU, about 8–12 s for a 4B-class model (173–195 tokens/s) and 3.5–8 s for a 2B-class model (260–435 tokens/s); cold on an old CPU at about 20–50 tokens/s, 30–100 s; with the prefix cached, only the new utterance (tens of tokens) is processed, giving roughly 1 s to the tool call and 2–4 s end to end, in line with the measured 0.87–1.15 s and 2.1–3.6 s. With a recent integrated GPU, even a cold 2k-token prompt would take about 0.5–1.7 s.
- The prompt must therefore be laid out for cache reuse: a byte-stable prefix (system instructions, tool schemas, the entity catalogue in a fixed order) followed by a short volatile tail (current state values, time, the utterance). Anything that changes per request, including the clock, belongs at the end; otherwise the whole prefix is reprocessed. Pinning the agent to one slot, warming it at service start and persisting the slot to disk avoids paying the cold cost after restarts.
- Entity state should not live in the prefix. Either inject only the few states relevant to the request at the tail or give the model a `get_state` tool; a catalogue of IDs and names changes rarely, live values change constantly.
- It is worth testing the integrated GPU before settling on pure CPU: the Lunar Lake figures show a seven- to ninefold prefill gain and about 1.5 times faster decoding, and the N150 report shows Vulkan offload working on a much weaker Intel iGPU. "No dedicated GPU" does not mean "no GPU acceleration" if the laptop is recent.
- A three-step local pipeline fits the evidence: (1) a deterministic matcher for the frequent, unambiguous commands in both languages, answering in milliseconds; (2) optionally a fine-tuned 270M function-calling model or a 1–2B model (Qwen3.5-2B, LFM3-1.7B) for single-shot commands the matcher misses; (3) a 4B-class model with the cached prefix for everything else, with thinking off. Each step either emits a validated tool call or declines and passes the request on.
- FunctionGemma is only attractive after fine-tuning on the home's own tool set and phrasing (including Russian), runs single-shot rather than as an agent, and is under Gemma terms rather than Apache 2.0; a rule-based matcher plus a 2–4B model may deliver most of the benefit with less work.
- Mixture-of-experts models are not automatically the right CPU choice. Speed depends on total memory footprint as well as active parameters: the 8B-total LFM2-8B-A1B was about 2.5 times faster than dense 4B models on an N150, but a 35B-total MoE was slower than a dense 4B on a server CPU. On a 16 GB machine only the 7–10B-total MoE models are candidates (Granite 4.0 H Tiny, LFM2.5-8B-A1B, GigaChat3.1-10B-A1.8B), and their CPU speed needs measuring.
- Memory plan for 16 GB: operating system and services about 3–4 GB; main model 2.7–6.5 GB; optional fast-path model 0.3–1.3 GB; speech recognition and synthesis about 0.7–1.5 GB; KV cache for an 8K context a few hundred megabytes. That leaves comfortable headroom with a 4B-class main model and little with a 10B MoE plus sidecars.
- Tiered operation should treat the Mac as an optional accelerator, never a dependency: probe its endpoint with a short timeout (a sleeping laptop fails fast only if connection timeouts are short), route rule-writing, multi-step and ambiguous requests to it when it answers, and fall back to the local model otherwise. Simple device commands should stay on the laptop even when the Mac is available, because the local cached path is already fast and avoids a network dependency for the most frequent action.
- Both tiers should expose the same OpenAI-compatible interface and identical tool schemas so that failover is a URL change; a small hand-written client with ordered endpoints and timeouts is enough, and LiteLLM is an off-the-shelf alternative. Deferred work (for example composing a complex automation) can be queued until the Mac is next reachable instead of being attempted by the small model.
- Because the two tiers differ in ability, the same private regression suite should be run against each, in both languages, and risky actions should be subject to the same code-side validation regardless of which model proposed them.

### Gaps
- The laptop's CPU model, memory configuration (single or dual channel) and integrated GPU are unknown, so all speed figures are analogies; running `llama-bench` and a real 1.5–2k-token prompt on the machine is the only way to size it.
- No published CPU measurements were found for Qwen3.5-4B on a laptop-class CPU with a long tool prompt, for GigaChat3.1 Lightning, T-lite-it-2.1, LFM3-1.7B or Granite 4.0 H Tiny under llama.cpp on CPU, or for FunctionGemma on x86.
- The time and file size of `llama-server` slot save and restore, and how well cache reuse works when the entity catalogue changes slightly, were not measured in any source found.
- LiteLLM's behaviour with a host that is asleep (connection timeouts rather than errors), and macOS wake-for-network behaviour when the Mac is closed, were not researched.
- Web search was unavailable for this section, so independent community reports on CPU-only voice assistants in late 2026 may have been missed.

## 11. Russian-language quality of candidate models and Russian-focused open-weight models (scope update)

### Takeaway
The best-documented Russian tool callers are T-Tech's Apache-2.0 fine-tunes of Qwen3 (T-lite-it-2.1 at 8B and T-pro-it-2.1 at 32B, December 2025), and the only Russian-first model that suits a CPU-only host by architecture is Sber's MIT-licensed GigaChat3.1-10B-A1.8B (10B total, 1.8B active, March 2026), which leads on Russian knowledge tests but has no published tool-calling score. Published Russian-versus-English function-calling pairs show Russian scoring about 5–12 points lower for the same model; the general candidates (Qwen3.5, Gemma 4, Nemotron Bolt E4B, LFM3) claim Russian support without Russian tool-calling numbers, YandexGPT 5 Lite carries a restrictive custom licence and no documented tool calling, and Vikhr's latest language models date from 2025 and report chat quality only.

- The Russian penalty is measurable and consistent: on the paired BFCL figures, Russian is lower than English by 5.7 points for T-lite-it-2.1, 6.8 for Qwen3-8B, 4.5 for Ministral 3 8B, 10.4 for A-vibe, 6.3 for T-pro-it-2.1, 11.9 for Qwen3-32B and 12.4 for gpt-oss-20b. A bilingual home agent should therefore be evaluated separately in each language, and a model that is adequate in English may need a larger size or a Russian fine-tune to match it in Russian.
- For the laptop, the practical shortlist to test head-to-head in both languages is Qwen3.5-4B (best general small tool caller), GigaChat3.1 Lightning (Russian-first, MIT, sparse) and LFM3-1.7B (smallest with Russian listed). T-lite-it-2.1 has the best Russian tool-calling evidence but is a dense 8B model and belongs on the Mac tier or in background tasks on this host.
- For the Mac tier, T-pro-it-2.1 is the best-evidenced Russian tool caller that fits 48 GB, but it is a Qwen3-generation, text-only, non-thinking model; the newer Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma 4 31B add vision and stronger agent benchmarks in English without Russian numbers. Running both on a private bilingual test set is the only way to choose.
- Entity naming for a bilingual home: give every device a stable ASCII identifier and attach Russian and English names and aliases as descriptions; let the model choose only among enumerated identifiers (enforced by the schema or grammar), so Cyrillic or mixed-language names are matched as text in the prompt but never have to be reproduced exactly in the tool call. Russian inflection (for example different case endings of the same room or device name) argues for alias lists or lemma-based matching in the deterministic fast path.
- Russian costs more tokens than English with general-purpose tokenizers (the Ruadapt authors report up to twice the generation speed after adapting the tokenizer), which on a 5–17 tokens/s CPU makes Russian replies noticeably slower. Having the model emit only the tool call and rendering the spoken confirmation from templates in code avoids most of that cost and keeps wording consistent in both languages.
- Speech recognition is likely to matter more than the language model for Russian commands. GigaAM-v3 is the strongest open option for Russian in the evidence found, but a bilingual household also needs English, which points to GigaAM-Multilingual, Qwen3-ASR or Parakeet v3, or to language identification followed by a per-language recogniser.
- Licences: Apache 2.0 and MIT models (Qwen, Gemma 4, T-Tech, GigaChat, Vikhr, Ruadapt) are unproblematic for a personal project; YandexGPT 5 Lite's agreement permits personal use but is non-transferable and conditional; LFM3 and the NVIDIA models carry vendor-specific licences that should be read before adoption; FunctionGemma is under Gemma terms.

### Gaps
- No Russian tool-calling results were found for Qwen3.5, Gemma 4, Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B, LFM3 or GigaChat3.1 Lightning; the only Russian function-calling numbers are T-Tech's own tables, which cover Qwen3-generation and a few other models.
- No evidence was found on tool calls with Cyrillic or mixed-language entity names, on Russian morphology in entity matching, or on Russian performance of FunctionGemma or the home-llm fine-tunes.
- The MERA Russian benchmark leaderboard could not be retrieved (404), so no independent cross-vendor Russian ranking is included; T-Tech's and Sber's tables are vendor-run.
- CPU speeds of the Russian-focused models (GigaChat3.1 Lightning, T-lite-it-2.1, QVikhr, Ruadapt) were not found; the Ruadapt speed-up is the authors' claim and was not independently measured.
- The licence texts of LFM3 ("LFM Open License v1.0") and of NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 models were not read; quantised sizes of T-pro-it-2.1 were not checked.
- Saiga-family models and any Russian fine-tunes of Gemma 4 or Qwen3.5 were not surveyed because web search was unavailable; Russian text-to-speech quality was not researched.
